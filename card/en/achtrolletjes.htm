ANP report on eight rolls of film, 28 November 2016

The passage

“The archive of the military security service MIVD is said to hold far more visual and audio material made by Dutchbat soldiers who recorded war crimes after the fall of Srebrenica. On behalf of what are by now more than two hundred members of Dutchbat III, the lawyers Klaas Arjen Krikke and Michael Ruperti therefore want to obtain access to the secret visual material by court order. This involves prints of at least eight rolls of film, they state.” Page 1, opening paragraph. Translated from the Dutch; the original wording is below.

Original wording

“In het archief van de militaire veiligheidsdienst MIVD moet veel meer beeld- en geluidsmateriaal liggen van Dutchbatters die oorlogsmisdaden hebben vastgelegd na de val van Srebrenica. Namens inmiddels ruim tweehonderd leden van Dutchbat III willen de advocaten Klaas Arjen Krikke en Michael Ruperti daarom via een gerechtelijk bevel inzage krijgen in het geheime beeldmateriaal. Het gaat om afdrukken van ten minste acht fotorolletjes, stellen zij.” The subheading above the report reads ‘Dutchbat II’, evidently a slip for Dutchbat III, which the body text names correctly.

Why this document matters

According to this ANP report of 28 November 2016, the lawyers Klaas Arjen Krikke and Michael Ruperti seek court-ordered access, on behalf of over two hundred members of Dutchbat III, to prints of at least eight rolls of film. They say these lie in the archive of the Military Intelligence and Security Service (MIVD), as the MID is now called. The essay cites this report to show that Rutten’s roll of film does not stand alone. Whether that claim was ever filed has never become public.
