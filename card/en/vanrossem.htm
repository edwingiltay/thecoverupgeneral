Historian Maar­ten van Ros­sem on Face­book, 12 April 2016

Three quotations in the Voices section come from this post: two from Van Rossem’s own text on page 1 and one from the comments beneath it, captured on page 2.

The post

“‘De Doof­pot­ge­ne­raal’ may be sold again after all, the Court of Ap­peal in The Hague ruled today. The book deals with the badly developed roll of film said to contain images of Bos­ni­an Mus­lims killed in Sre­bre­ni­ca. In this issue we speak to Joris Voor­hoe­ve, then Min­is­ter of De­fence, about the matter. He comes up with a sur­pris­ing answer: ‘Far more important is the lost fax with 239 names of Mus­lim men.’” The post in full, apart from the closing link. Translated from the Dutch; the original wording is below. De doof­pot­ge­ne­raal is the book’s original Dutch title; the English edition is The Cover-up Gen­eral. The post opens the quotation from Voor­hoe­ve with a typographic quotation mark and closes it with a straight apostrophe. After it, still on the same line, comes the link http://bit.ly/1VO2jDQ followed by (€), the sign that the article is behind a paywall.

“‘De Doof­pot­ge­ne­raal’ mag toch weer verkocht worden, zo besloot het ge­rechts­hof Den Haag vandaag. Het boek handelt over het verkeerd ont­wik­kel­de fo­to­rol­le­tje waarop beelden zouden staan van om­ge­ko­men Bos­ni­sche moslims in Sre­bre­ni­ca. In dit nummer spreken we Joris Voor­hoe­ve, destijds minister van Defensie, over de kwestie. Hij komt met een ver­ras­send antwoord: ‘Veel be­lang­rij­ker is de zoek­ge­raak­te fax met 239 namen van mos­lim­man­nen.’” Original wording

Why this document matters

On the day the Court of Appeal of The Hague ruled that the book could be sold again, the historian Maar­ten van Ros­sem passed the news on to his readers. In the same post he pointed to an interview with Joris Voor­hoe­ve, Min­is­ter of De­fence at the time of the fall of Sre­bre­ni­ca, who answered: ‘Far more important is the lost fax with 239 names of Mus­lim men.’ Three of the quotations in the Voices section come from this single post: one on the release of the book, one with Voor­hoe­ve’s answer about another missing piece of Sre­bre­ni­ca evidence, and one from the comments beneath it, where Bojan Bre­lja­ko­vić writes that he bought the book at Athenaeum while it was still banned. That purchase falls between the District Court’s judgment of 14 December 2015 and the ruling of the Court of Appeal: the judgment ordered the author to stop distributing the book, but imposed no recall of copies already in bookshops.
