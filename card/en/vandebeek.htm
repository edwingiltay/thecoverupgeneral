Blog post quoting Eric van de Beek, 11 Feb­ru­ary 2016

The passage

“The former spy claims she is described incorrectly in De doof­pot­ge­ne­raal. Journalist Eric van de Beek (ericvandebeek.nl): ‘I was present at the trial. The MID lady provided no shred of evidence for her claim that what Giltay writes about her is made up.’” Fourth paragraph of the post. Translated from the Dutch; the original wording is below. De doof­pot­ge­ne­raal is the book’s original Dutch title; the English edition is The Cover-up Gen­eral.

Original wording

“De ex-spionne beweert onjuist te zijn beschreven in De doof­pot­ge­ne­raal. Journalist Eric van de Beek (ericvandebeek.nl): ‘Ik was bij de rechtszaak aanwezig. De MID-mevrouw leverde geen splinter bewijs voor haar claim dat wat Giltay over haar schrijft uit de duim is gezogen.’” ‘MID’ is the Militaire Inlichtingendienst, the Dutch Military Intelligence Service. The post introduces the speaker simply as ‘Journalist Eric van de Beek’.

Why this document matters

This post is the source of Eric van de Beek’s statement in the Voices section. He was in the courtroom for the case that led to the ban, and he reports that the former MID employee Barbara Overduyn produced no evidence at all for her claim that what the book says about her was invented. The same post records that Giltay had submitted his manuscript to the Minister of Defence for comment in March 2014, eight months before the book appeared, and that the Minister raised no objections.
