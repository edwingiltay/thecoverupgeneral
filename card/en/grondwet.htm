Constitution, Article 45 on the coherence of government policy, text of 14 March 2023

The passage

“1. The Ministers shall together constitute the Cabinet. 2. The Prime Minister shall chair the Cabinet. 3. The Cabinet shall consider and decide upon overall government policy and shall promote the coherence thereof.” Page 9, under the heading ‘Artikel 45’, in Chapter 2 (Government), § 2 (The King and the Ministers). The three paragraphs each stand on a line of their own in the Staatsblad and are run together here. The English wording is the official translation published by the Ministry of the Interior and Kingdom Relations (The Constitution of the Kingdom of the Netherlands 2023), which renders ‘ministerraad’ as ‘Cabinet’; the Dutch text is below.

Original wording

“1. De ministers vormen te zamen de ministerraad. 2. De minister-president is voorzitter van de ministerraad. 3. De ministerraad beraadslaagt en besluit over het algemeen regeringsbeleid en bevordert de eenheid van dat beleid.” The Dutch text as it stands in the Staatsblad; the word ‘regeringsbeleid’ is hyphenated across two lines there.

Why this document matters

Article 45 of the Dutch Constitution provides that the Ministers together constitute the Cabinet (ministerraad) and that this body considers and decides upon overall government policy and promotes the coherence of that policy. The provision has been in the Constitution since the general revision of 1983; the Rules of Procedure of the Council of Ministers repeat it in Article 4 and add, in Article 12(2), that under no circumstances does a minister or state secretary act against a decision of the Council. The essay sets this constitutional duty beside two statements by members of the same cabinet about the same book: in January 2021 the Minister of Justice and Security wrote that the labels of fake news, conspiracy theory and anti-government propaganda were “in no way based” on the content of the book, whereas in August 2018 the Minister of Defence had dismissed it as a book in which fact and fiction become intertwined. The article does not prescribe what ministers should think of a book, but it makes the coherence of government policy a constitutional duty, and it is against that duty that the essay measures the failure to speak with one voice.
