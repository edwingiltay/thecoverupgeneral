Bar­ba­ra Over­duyn’s writ of summons against Giltay, 2015

The passage

“2. Overduyn then ordered the book and read it with mounting dismay and anger, and then first contacted her former employer the Ministry of Defence, which, like her, is equally involved in the content of the book. Now, the Ministry of Defence did turn out to be aware of the book, but to have decided to take no action against it, without realising that the Overduyn who appears frequently in the book was a former employee of its own, whom the Ministry accordingly asked nothing.” Page 2, paragraph 2. Translated from the Dutch; the original wording is below.

Original wording

“2. Overduyn heeft toen het boek besteld en dat met de toenemende ontsteltenis en boosheid gelezen om vervolgens eerst contact op te nemen met haar ex-werkgever Defensie, net als zij evenzeer betrokken bij de inhoud van het boek. Welnu, Defensie bleek het boek wel te kennen, maar besloten te hebben daartegen geen actie te nemen zonder te beseffen dat de in het boek frequent voorkomende Overduyn een ex-medewerker van haar was aan wie Defensie dan ook niets heeft gevraagd.” In the writ the word ‘ex-medewerker’ is broken across two lines. The paragraph continues after the quoted passage with ‘Het leed was dus al geschied, zonder dat Overduyn iets tegen de verschijning van het boek had’ and then runs on to a page that is not part of this extract.

Why this document matters

In her writ of 2015, Barbara Overduyn states that after reading the book she first approached her former employer, the Ministry of Defence, and that the Ministry turned out to be aware of the book but had decided to take no action against it, without realising that the Overduyn who appears in it so often had once worked for the Ministry. What the essay says on this point rests on this document. She then demanded a full publication and speaking ban, which the District Court imposed in December 2015.
