Ca­se­ma cer­tif­i­cate of em­ploy­ment for Giltay, 8 June 1999

The passage

“I hereby declare that Mr E. Giltay, born on […] 1970 and residing at Reepstraat 5, 2583 XE Scheveningen, worked from 8 June 1998 to 23 July 1998 as a temporary worker at N.V. Casema, Brassersplein 2, Delft, in the position of Telesales employee in the Consumer Market Sales department. […] The person concerned performed these duties properly.” Page 1, the opening sentence and the closing line of the certificate. The quotation omits the list of Telesales duties between that sentence and the closing line; ‘these duties’ are those tasks. The day and month of birth are blacked out in the PDF. Translated from the Dutch; the original wording is below.

Original wording

“Hierdoor verklaar ik dat de heer E. Giltay geboren op […] 1970 en wonende Reepstraat 5, 2583 XE te Scheveningen van 8 juni 1998 tot 23 juli 1998 als uitzendkracht werkzaam is geweest bij de N.V. Casema, Brassersplein 2 te Delft, in de functie van medewerker Telesales bij de afdeling Verkoop Consumentenmarkt. […] Betrokkene heeft deze werkzaamheden naar behoren uitgevoerd.”

Why this document matters

In this certificate of 8 June 1999 Casema states that Giltay was engaged as a temporary worker from 8 June to 23 July 1998, as a Telesales employee, and closes with the sentence “The person concerned performed these duties properly.” The essay sets such references from the companies he had worked for against the characterisation of Giltay that the Minister of Defence put on record in 1999 and never withdrew, and which is still online in the National Ombudsman’s report 1999/507. That report states that Giltay had supposedly been dismissed by Casema for “irritating and maladjusted behaviour”. This certificate is the company’s own account of the same weeks the Minister described very differently.
