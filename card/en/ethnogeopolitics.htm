Forum of EthnoGeoPolitics, on the failings of the Ministry of Defence, Summer 2018

The passage

“Having been discredited by the Ministry is one thing—Giltay thinks there are bigger fish to fry: The covering up of the truth about Srebrenica. ‘The Ministry never quite developed admitting mistakes. Unlike certain film rolls.’ De Bruijne adds: ‘They prefer to keep everything under wraps, lest damages claims will ensue. Neither justice nor truth are relevant to them.’” Page 53 of the journal, the closing paragraph of the section headed ‘Discredited’. This document is itself the English trans­la­tion, so the wording above is the pub­lished English text and no separate original is quoted below. The article puts the spoken words in double quo­ta­tion marks; they are set as single ones here, inside the outer quo­ta­tion.

Why this document matters

The quo­ta­tion in the Voices section is the closing line of this guest column, rendered from the Dutch original on Novini. Here it appears in the published English translation. Dutch­bat veteran Remko de Bruijne, who was in the enclave in 1995 and who says he has been in legal battles with the Min­is­try for fourteen years, explains why, in his view, Defence keeps everything under wraps: doing so keeps damages claims at bay. On the same page the Minister of Defence, Ank Bijleveld, says, when asked, that fact and fiction are indeed in­ter­twined in the book. The article then states that the Court of Appeal of The Hague over­turned the ban and ruled that the ac­cu­ra­cy of the book is not in doubt. The judgment itself says that the description of the former employee in the book ‘at least partly finds support in the facts’ (para. 4.10) and that the inaccuracies are insufficient to call the author’s care into question (para. 4.9). The piece appeared here in English in the journal Forum of EthnoGeoPolitics, six days after the Dutch original.
