Ministry of the Interior to Giltay, 5 Feb­ru­ary 2015

The essay quotes this letter’s refusal to respond substantively to the manuscript. The same letter announces that Giltay’s complaint about the AIVD will be put before the CTIVD. Both passages are given below in the order of the letter.

No substantive response

“On 18 March 2014 you sent me the manuscript of your book ‘De doofpotgeneraal’ and asked me for a response. In your email of 1 February 2014 you state that you expect a written and substantive response to your letter of 18 March 2014. In reply I can tell you that I have taken note of your letter and the manuscript. I shall not respond substantively to this any further.” Page 1, the second paragraph of the letter. The letter dates Giltay’s email 1 February 2014, whereas its opening paragraph refers to emails of 29 December 2014 and 1 February 2015. De doof­pot­ge­ne­raal is the book’s original Dutch title; the English edition is The Cover-up Gen­eral. Translated from the Dutch; the original wording is below.

“Op 18 maart 2014 heeft u mij het manuscript van uw boek ‘De doofpotgeneraal’ toegezonden en mij om een reactie gevraagd. In uw email van 1 februari 2014 geeft u aan een schriftelijke en inhoudelijke reactie te verwachten op uw brief van 18 maart 2014. Ik kan u hierop aangeven dat ik kennis heb genomen van uw brief en het manuscript. Ik zal er verder niet inhoudelijk op reageren.” Original wording

The complaint goes to the CTIVD

“In accordance with your request, your complaint will be put before the CTIVD. This independent committee will be asked to advise on your complaint. For its investigation the committee has direct access to all data held by the AIVD.” Page 1, the third paragraph of the letter, its second to fourth sentences. The paragraph opens by acknowledging receipt of the complaint and closes by referring to the committee itself for the procedure. Translated from the Dutch; the original wording is below.

“Conform uw verzoek zal uw klacht aan de CTIVD worden voorgelegd. Deze onafhankelijke commissie wordt gevraagd te adviseren ten aanzien van uw klacht. Voor haar onderzoek heeft de commissie rechtstreekse toegang tot alle gegevens waarover de AIVD beschikt.” Original wording

Why this document matters

In this letter of 5 February 2015, more than ten months after Giltay had delivered his letter with the manuscript, the Minister of the Interior writes that he has taken note of both: “I shall not respond substantively to this any further.” The essay’s table of the State’s responses to the book lists this answer from the Minister, Ronald Plasterk, who was responsible for the successor to the BVD, under 2015. There it counts as one of seven moments at which a minister was formally notified.

The same letter grants Giltay’s request to put his complaint about the AIVD before the CTIVD, the Review Committee on the Intelligence and Security Services. That complaint concerned his suspicion that the AIVD had intercepted his communications with the lawyer Britta Böhler. The suspicion arose after it had emerged that the service had eavesdropped on the law firm Prakken d’Oliveira. His supplementary complaint about the affair described in the book also went to the CTIVD, as the Ministry’s letter of 2 March 2015 shows, but forty-six days later, by letter of 17 April 2015, the Minister decided after all not to consider that complaint.
