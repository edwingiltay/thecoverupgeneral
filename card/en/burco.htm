Exhibit · Article

Burco Online News, Banned Books, 22 March 2018

The passage

“How to deal with books in which forbidden matters are found, forbidden by custom, religion or law. All copies of the book must be collected and burned or destroyed. If the copies are held on a computer, in a system or on tape, they must be erased. And the reprinting of those faulty books must be prohibited. Once they have been corrected, with whatever was wrong thrown out, they must be reprinted.” Opening paragraph of the article, under the standfirst ‘How To Deal With Censored Books, As Having Harmful Content’. Trans­lat­ed from the So­ma­li; the original wording is below.

Original wording

“Si loo xalliyo buugta, laga helo waxyaabo reeban; ku reeban dhaqan, diin amma sharci. Waa in la soo ururiyaa dhammaan nuqulada buugaa, lana gubaa amma la baa-biiyaa. Haddii ay yihiin nuqulo kumbuyuuter , system amma cajalad ku jirana waan in la-masaxo. Lana mamnuuco in dib loo daabaco buugtaa qaldan. Iyaga oo saxan, lagana tuuray wixii qaldanaa waa in dib loo daabacaa.” Quoted from the article as archived by the Internet Archive on 26 March 2018. The spelling, spacing and hyphenation are the paper’s own, among them ‘kumbuyuuter ,’ with a space before the comma, and the hyphenated forms ‘baa-biiyaa’ and ‘la-masaxo’.

Why this document matters

More than two years after the Dutch ban, and almost two years after it was lifted, the book was still being listed as banned far beyond Europe. Burco Online News, a local Somaliland newspaper, first sets out what should happen to books found to contain forbidden content: every copy must be collected and burned or destroyed, and digital copies must be erased. A list of banned books worldwide then follows. The Cover-up General appears on that list beside Salman Rushdie’s The Satanic Verses and the Bible, and is recorded as banned in the Netherlands in 2015.
