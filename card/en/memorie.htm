Over­duyn’s state­ment of de­fence to the Court of Ap­peal of The Hague, 2 Feb­ru­a­ry 2016

The passage

“36. Giltay further com­plains that Over­duyn’s position is that he had allegedly been dismissed from Casema for mal­ad­just­ed militant be­hav­iour. He says she advanced that wrongly, because it has since been refuted in writing by Giltay with sup­port­ing evidence. The Dis­trict Court paid no at­ten­tion to it, but this was no dif­fer­ent from the in­for­ma­tion Over­duyn had, and so neither this fact nor Gil­tay’s re­fu­ta­tion was im­por­tant for the judg­ment.” Page 11 of the statement of defence, paragraph 36, the second page of this extract. Translated from the Dutch; the original wording is below.

Original wording

“36. Dan be­klaagt Giltay zich er nog over dat de stel­ling van Over­duyn is dat hij bij Ca­se­ma zou zijn ont­sla­gen we­gens on­aan­ge­past mi­li­tant ge­drag. Zij zou dat ten on­rech­te heb­ben aan­ge­voerd, om­dat dit in­tus­sen schrif­te­lijk met pro­duc­ties door Giltay is weer­legd. De recht­bank heeft daar geen aan­dacht aan be­steed, maar dit was niet an­ders dan de in­for­ma­tie die Over­duyn had en­be­lang­rijk voor het oor­deel was dit feit en de weer­leg­ging van Giltay dan ook niet.” The document itself runs two words together as “enbelangrijk”, for “en belangrijk”; the reading is “and so neither this fact nor Giltay’s refutation was important for the judgment”. The rest of both pages has been blacked out, so that only the letterhead details and paragraph 36 remain visible.

Why this document matters

Over­duyn was the Min­is­try’s chief witness against Giltay, and her claim that he, placed at Ca­se­ma as a Randstad temp, had been dis­missed for “irritating and mal­ad­just­ed be­hav­iour” became the Min­is­ter’s position in the Na­tion­al Om­buds­man’s public report of 1999. In this statement of de­fence, seventeen years later, her counsel sets out Giltay’s com­plaint and leaves its core un­chal­lenged.

The clause “because it has since been refuted in writing by Giltay with sup­port­ing evidence” is in the in­dic­a­tive, not “has allegedly been refuted” or “according to Giltay”, and in the next sentence the statement of defence itself speaks of “Giltay’s re­fu­ta­tion”. The de­fence is not that the claim was true, but that it was “no dif­fer­ent from the in­for­ma­tion Over­duyn had” and that neither the fact nor the re­fu­ta­tion mattered for the judg­ment. Under Dutch civil pro­ce­dure that is de­ci­sive: facts asserted by one party and not, or not suf­fi­cient­ly, disputed by the other must be treated by the court as es­tab­lished (article 149 of the Code of Civil Pro­ce­dure). In these pro­ceed­ings, then, it was es­tab­lished that the dis­miss­al claim had been refuted. The quo­ta­tion in the Voices section sums this up as leaving the re­fu­ta­tion un­chal­lenged after seventeen years. The char­ac­ter­i­sa­tion of Giltay in the Ombudsman’s report has never been withdrawn by the Min­is­try.
