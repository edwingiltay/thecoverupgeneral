Leidsch Dagblad on the book launch, 27 No­vem­ber 2014

The passage

“You notice it every time government information goes missing, such as a crucial intercepted telephone call […] only last year. Then everyone immediately shouts: ‘See, just like that film roll’. The moment it is decided that something must stay secret, the wheels start turning. Why can’t the government simply be open? It’s important that this riddle, too, is solved once and for all.” Page 1 of the clipping, the closing paragraph under the subheading ‘Open zenuw’ (Raw nerve). Translated from the Dutch; the original wording is below.

Original wording

“Dat merk je elke keer dat er overheidsinformatie zoek raakt, zoals vorig jaar nog een cruciaal tapgesprek […]. Dan roept iedereen direct: ‘zie je wel, net als dat fotorolletje’. Zodra wordt besloten dat iets geheim moet blijven, begint er een molen te draaien. Waarom kan de overheid niet gewoon open zijn? Het is belangrijk dat ook dit raadsel voorgoed wordt opgelost.” The clipping is set in one narrow column with hyphenated line breaks; in the wording above, those breaks have been closed up. The paper opens De Winter’s words with the Dutch low double quotation mark and uses single quotes inside them. The omitted words name the parties to that intercepted call, which belongs to a different affair.

Why this document matters

In this clipping about the launch in No­vem­ber 2014, in­ves­ti­ga­tive journalist Bren­no de Win­ter, who accepted the first copy, says: “Why can’t the government simply be open? It’s important that this riddle, too, is solved once and for all.” The same column reports that former Min­is­ter Jan Pronk called the book ‘well doc­u­ment­ed’. The clipping stands at the start of the press attention that the essay reads not as a tribute but as a question to the Ministry of Defence: what did the Mil­i­tary In­tel­li­gence Ser­vice have to hide?
