Nieuwe Revu, four-star review, 4 Feb­ru­ary 2015

The passage

“Temp work, bungling managers, failing controls, and the country’s intelligence services as the icing on top: these are the ingredients of the true crime thriller De Doofpotgeneraal. How General Ad van Baal has his wife infiltrate the call centre where author Edwin Giltay works.” Opening of the review, page 1 of the clipping. Translated from the Dutch; the original wording is below. De doof­pot­ge­ne­raal is the book’s original Dutch title; the English edition is The Cover-up Gen­eral.

Original wording

“Uitzendwerk, klungelende leidinggevenden, falende controles, maar dan nog met ’s lands inlichtingendiensten als extra toefje: het zijn de ingrediënten van de true crime-thriller De Doofpotgeneraal. Hoe Generaal Ad van Baal zijn vrouw laat infiltreren in het callcenter waar auteur Edwin Giltay werkt.” The review is set in narrow columns with hyphenated line breaks; in the wording above, those breaks have been closed up. The apostrophe in ’s lands is printed as a straight typewriter apostrophe.

Why this document matters

Nieu­we Revu, a Dutch opinion weekly, gave De doof­pot­ge­ne­raal four stars and read it as a true crime thriller about a sorry tribal feud at the top of the army over the Sre­bre­ni­ca film roll. Six months later Barbara Overduyn, a former military intelligence employee, demanded that copies be recalled, and in De­cem­ber 2015 the court banned the book. The clipping records how the book was being read in the Dutch press before that claim reached the court. The essay counts this review as part of the critical acclaim the book received in 2015.
