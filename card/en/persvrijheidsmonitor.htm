Exhibit · Record

Villamedia, Press Free­dom Mon­i­tor, 12 April 2016

The passage

“The Court of Appeal of The Hague has quashed a distribution ban on the book ‘De doofpotgeneraal’ imposed at the end of last year. The book, which deals with the notorious film roll from Sebrenica, was said to damage the good name of a former employee of the Military Intelligence Service (MID).” The full text of the entry, below the headline. Translated from the Dutch; the original wording is below. The monitor spells ‘Sebrenica’ without the r.

Original wording

“Het gerechtshof Den Haag heeft een eind vorig jaar opgelegd verspreidingsverbod van het boek ‘De doofpotgeneraal’ vernietigd. Het boek, dat handelt over het beruchte fotorolletje uit Sebrenica, zou een oud-medewerkster van de Militaire Inlichtingendienst (MID) in haar goede naam aantasten.” The entry is written in Dutch.

Why this document matters

This places the book ban in the press-freedom record kept by Dutch journalism itself. The Persvrijheidsmonitor (‘press freedom monitor’) tracks developments in press freedom in the Netherlands and is supported by the Persvrijheidsfonds foundation, set up in 2007 by the Dutch Association of Journalists and the Society of Editors-in-Chief. The entry of 12 April 2016 sums up the judgment: the Court of Appeal of The Hague quashed the distribution ban imposed in late 2015 on the ground that the book was said to damage the good name of a former employee of the Military Intelligence Service. Where Mapping Media Freedom logged the ban as an international violation, this monitor records how it ended at home.
