Slobodna Bosna, interview with Giltay on the withheld pho­to­graphs, 21 July 2018

The passage

“The inertia of the Dutch soldiers was part of a Dutch military strategy. As Edwin Giltay, the author of the book The Cover-up General, puts it, they ‘left’ the Bosniak population of Srebrenica to the soldiers of the Army of the Republika Srpska. In those dramatic moments the Dutch officer Johannes Rutten took photographs showing nine Bosniak corpses, which is evidence of when the genocide began, because the pictures were taken on 13 July, that is, only two days after the fall of Srebrenica.” Page 1, the second paragraph below the photograph, part of the introduction before the interview begins. Trans­lat­ed from the Bos­ni­an; the original wording is below.

Original wording

“Inertnost holandskih vojnika bila je dio holandske vojne strategije koji su, kako kaže Edwin Giltay, pisac knjige The Cover – up General, bošnjačko stanovnštvo Srebrenice „prepustili“ vojnicima Vojske RS-a. U tim dramatičnim trenucima holandski oficir Johannes Rutten napravio je fotografije na kojima se vidi devet bošnjačkih leševa, što je dokaz vremenskog početka genocida, jer su snimci napravljeni 13. jula, dakle samo dva dana nakon pada Srebrenice.” The paragraph runs over four lines on the printed page; in the text above, the line breaks have been closed up. The article prints the title of the book as The Cover – up General, with a spaced dash, and the translation gives it as The Cover-up General. The article writes stanovnštvo for stanovništvo.

Why this document matters

On 21 July 2018 Slobodna Bosna runs the headline that the Netherlands went to great lengths to hide the pho­to­graphs, and its introduction sets out what those pictures show: nine Bosniak bodies, photographed by the Dutch officer Johannes Rutten on 13 July 1995, two days after the fall of Sre­bre­ni­ca. According to the article, this is evidence of the moment the genocide began. The interview that follows runs from the rolls of film handed in for developing, through the secret meeting at which it was argued that the pictures should never be made public, to the ban on The Cover-up General and the reversal of that ban by the Court of Appeal of The Hague. The essay counts the article as part of the sustained attention the case drew abroad between 2018 and 2023.
