David Vogel on LinkedIn about the gag order, 21 Feb­ru­ary 2024

The passage

“Globally, the use of gag orders isn’t uncommon. In the Netherlands, for example, a court issued a gag order on writer Edwin Giltay over his book on a Dutch military intelligence scandal, an order that was subsequently revoked.” Page 2 of the printout, the paragraph beginning ‘Globally, the use of gag orders’.

Why this document matters

This article shows that the 2015 gag order is also cited as an example in the American debate on freedom of expression. The occasion is the gag order on Donald Trump in the criminal case over his attempts to overturn the result of the 2020 presidential election. To show that the tension between judicial proceedings and free speech is not a uniquely American issue, Vogel mentions the order a Dutch court imposed on Giltay, and he notes that it was later revoked. He writes in a personal capacity; the article ends by recommending his own solar energy fund.
