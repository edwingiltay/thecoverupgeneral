The death

“Death · Date of death: 19-11-2024 · Place of death: Rijswijk · Country of death: Nederland [the Netherlands]” The ‘Overlijden’ block on page 1, the heading and the three lines that record the death. Why this document matters

This record sets down her official name: Barbara Vivienne Overduyn. That matters because the spelling of her name was one of the two inaccuracies in the book that, according to the Court of Appeal of The Hague in 2016, were undisputed. The book used Overduin, the spelling found in the MID’s internal investigation of 12 Feb­ru­ary 1999. She herself signed a fax to the National Ombudsman as Overduijn that same year, and the court found that in it she did not spell her own name correctly. The record is an extract from the Dutch pop­u­la­tion register, drawn from the files of the last mu­ni­ci­pal­i­ty where she lived. It also records that she died on 19 No­vem­ber 2024 in Rijs­wijk; that year is the basis for the essay’s remark that the Min­is­try of De­fence continues to maintain the position of its chief witness even after her death. Her parents’ details, her reg­is­tra­tion and every address are blacked out.
