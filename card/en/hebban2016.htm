Hebban, Court of Appeal overturns book ban, 12 April 2016

The passage

“‘Banning an entire book almost never happens’, says Giltay’s lawyer Jurian van Groenendaal. ‘It remains a mystery why the book was banned in the first place. But apparently De Doofpotgeneraal was considered too explosive.’” Fourth paragraph of the article. Translated from the Dutch; the original wording is below. De doof­pot­ge­ne­raal is the book’s original Dutch title; the English edition is The Cover-up Gen­eral.

Original wording

“‘Het verbieden van een heel boek komt eigenlijk nooit voor’, zegt Giltays raadsman Jurian van Groenendaal. ‘Het is nog steeds een raadsel waarom het boek in eerste instantie werd verboden. Maar kennelijk werd De Doofpotgeneraal te brisant gevonden.’” Inside the quotation Hebban capitalises the title as ‘De Doofpotgeneraal’, while spelling it ‘De doofpotgeneraal’ elsewhere in the piece.

Why this document matters

This news item records the moment the ban was lifted. It reports that the Court of Appeal of The Hague set aside the publication ban imposed in preliminary relief proceedings at the request of a former Military Intelligence Service employee described in the book (Barbara Overduyn, whom the report does not name). It carries the reaction of Giltay’s lawyer Jurian van Groenendaal, who says that banning an entire book almost never happens and that the reason for the ban remains unexplained. The piece also notes that the former employee offered no evidence for her claim that the book contained untruths.
