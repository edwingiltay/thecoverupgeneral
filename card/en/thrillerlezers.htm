Thrillerlezersblog on the demand for a ban, 6 August 2015

The passage

“According to the former MID employee the book contains many errors that damage her name and her reputation. She also says the subject of Srebrenica is so sensitive that her safety is at stake. As if the MID had not already ruined its own reputation with all the lying and spinning over the roll of film, for instance, I think to myself. Instead of finally coming clean about what went on back then, they fuss about a book.” Second paragraph of the post, from its second sentence onwards. Translated from the Dutch; the original wording is below.

Original wording

“Volgens de voormalig MID-medewerker staan er veel fouten in het boek, die haar naam en reputatie aantasten. Ook is volgens haar het onderwerp Srebrenica zo gevoelig dat haar veiligheid in het geding is. Alsof de MID niet zelf al voor hun slechte naam gezorgd heeft met al het gelieg en gedraai rondom bijvoorbeeld het fotorolletje, denk ik dan. In plaats dat er eindelijk eens volledige openheid wordt gegeven over wat daar toen speelde, maakt men zich druk om een boek.” The post spells out the abbreviation in its opening line, ‘de voorheen Militaire Inlichtingendienst (MID)’. It does not name the former employee.

Why this document matters

This post by Thrillerlezersblog, one of the media voices gathered on this site, reports that a former employee of the Military Intelligence Service (Barbara Overduyn, whom the post does not name) wants De doof­pot­ge­ne­raal withdrawn from sale, sold copies recalled and a rectification published, and that Giltay denies the book contains factual errors. The blogger then sets that demand against the service’s own record on the roll of film and finds the priorities the wrong way round: a fuss is being made about a book while what happened back then has still not been explained.
