De Volkskrant, front page on the re­moval of the MID lead­er­ship, 14 July 1999

The passage

“Defence Minister De Grave has pushed aside the leadership of the Mili­tary In­tel­li­gence Ser­vice (MID). He accuses the MID leadership of having made a ‘serious error of judgement’ by failing to inform him of an in­ves­ti­ga­tion of its own into right-wing extremist conduct by four Dutchbat soldiers in Sre­bre­ni­ca, he said on Tuesday. After a ‘searching’ con­ver­sa­tion with the Minister, both the head of the MID, Air Force General H. Van­de­weij­er, and his deputy R. Wielinga offered to step down. In an ex­plan­a­to­ry statement De Grave stressed that the two had not been dismissed. ‘The sanction is adequate.’” Front page, the opening paragraph and the start of the second paragraph of the report. Translated from the Dutch; the original wording is below.

Original wording

“Minister De Grave van Defensie heeft de leiding van de Mi­li­tai­re In­lich­tin­gen­dienst (MID) aan de kant geschoven. Hij verwijt de MID-top een ‘ernstige be­oor­de­lings­fout’ te hebben gemaakt door hem niet op de hoogte te stellen van een eigen onderzoek naar rechts-ex­tre­mis­tisch gedrag van vier Dutchbat-mi­li­tai­ren in Sre­bre­ni­ca, zei hij dinsdag. Na een ‘in­drin­gend’ geprek met de minister heeft zowel het hoofd van de MID, lucht­macht­ge­ne­raal H. Van­de­weij­er, als zijn plaats­ver­van­ger R. Wielinga zijn functie ter be­schik­king gesteld. De Grave be­na­druk­te in een toe­lich­ting dat de twee niet zijn ontslagen. ‘De sanctie is adequaat.’” The paper itself prints ‘geprek’ for ‘gesprek’, a typesetting slip that has been left as it stands. The report is set in narrow columns with hyphenated line breaks; those breaks have been closed up in the text above.

Why this document matters

This front page of de Volkskrant reports that Minister De Grave “pushed aside” the leadership of the Military Intelligence Service over a “serious error of judgement”. The essay states that this happened two weeks after Giltay reported the ministerial statements about him as slander and libel to the Chief Public Prosecutor in The Hague, Van Gend.

The Minister’s complaint was that the service had not informed him of its own 1996 in­ves­ti­ga­tion into right-wing extremist conduct by four Dutchbat III soldiers in Sre­bre­ni­ca. Nor had the service acted on his call of August 1998 to place all Sre­bre­ni­ca material at the disposal of the Queen’s Com­mis­sion­er Jos van Kemenade, who was examining whether Defence had withheld information. According to the paper, the head and his deputy offered to step down, and the Minister stressed that they had not been dismissed and called the sanction adequate.
