Schrijven Online, book ban lifted on appeal, 12 April 2016

The passage

“What stood out was the lack of evidence the respondent brought forward: ‘She was completely unable to substantiate her dubious accusations. All we heard was: “None of it is true!” Showing how it actually was, not a chance. Pure bluffing,’ says Giltay.” Page 1, under the heading ‘Hoger beroep’, the first paragraph. Translated from the Dutch; the original wording is below.

Original wording

“Opvallend was het gebrek aan bewijsmateriaal wat de geïntimideerde inbracht: ‘Ze wist haar dubieuze beschuldigingen absoluut niet te staven. Het enige wat we hoorden, was: ‘Er klopt helemaal niets van!’ Aantonen hoe het dan wél zou zitten – ho maar. Pure blufpoker,’ aldus Giltay.” The article writes ‘geïntimideerde’ (intimidated) where the legal term is ‘geïntimeerde’ (respondent), and it is left as printed; the wording was corrected on the website later. The quotation within Giltay’s words carries single quotation marks in the original; here it is in double quotation marks.

Why this document matters

On the day the ban fell, the trade platform of Schrijven Magazine reported the outcome to the Dutch writing profession itself, having followed the case from the hearing onwards. The article looks back at the appeal hearing of 25 February 2016 and records what stood out there: how little evidence the other side put forward (Barbara Overduyn, whom the article does not name). In the article Giltay says that she was unable to substantiate her accusations. It also carries the reaction of his counsel Jurian van Groenendaal, who says that banning an entire book almost never happens and that the reason for this ban remains a mystery.
