Haarlems Weekblad, review, 27 May 2015

The passage

“You might think it was a bad joke, but it really happened. […] In a sober writing style with an eye for detail, Edwin Giltay describes in De doof­pot­ge­ne­raal the bungling conduct of two ill-mannered spies that he witnessed.” Page 1, opening sentence of the first body paragraph and the closing sentence of the second. Translated from the Dutch; the original wording is below. De doof­pot­ge­ne­raal is the book’s original Dutch title; the English edition is The Cover-up Gen­eral.

Original wording

“Je zou denken aan een slechte grap maar het is echt gebeurd. […] In een nuchtere schrijfstijl met oog voor detail beschrijft Edwin Giltay in De doof­pot­ge­ne­raal het het klungelige optreden van twee spionnen met gebrekkige omgangsvormen waarvan hij getuige was.” The article is set in narrow columns with hyphenated line breaks; those breaks have been closed up in the wording above. The doubled ‘het het’ is printed that way in the paper, and the paper spells the book title De doof­pot­ge­ne­raal, with a lower-case d.

Why this document matters

Haar­lems Week­blad, the local weekly of Haar­lem, wrote on 27 May 2015 that the story might seem like a bad joke but really happened. The piece followed Giltay’s guest appearance at the debate café of the Pletterij, and also reviewed the book.

The paper set out what had followed the incident involving the two spies: a complaint to the National Ombudsman, a copy sent to the Ministry of Defence for comment, which drew no reply at all, and a promised investigation that was called off a month after it was announced. The essay counts this piece as part of the critical acclaim the book received in 2015. This review is an example of how the book was being read in the Dutch press months before a court banned it.
