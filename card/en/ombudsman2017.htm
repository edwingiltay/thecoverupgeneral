National Ombudsman to Giltay, 7 March 2017

The passage

“The Court of Appeal has taken a decision on your appeal against the ban on further publication/distribution of your book. In its judgment the Court of Appeal declared Mrs Overduyn’s objections to further publication/distribution unfounded. The Court of Appeal makes no further pronouncement on report 1999/507 of the National Ombudsman, or on whether Mrs Overduyn did have access to files at the MID. The statement of defence of Mrs Overduyn’s counsel, too, expressly denies that she had access to the files in question. In view of the above, we will not revisit our report on your complaint.” Page 2, the second paragraph of the letter. Translated from the Dutch; the original wording is below.

Original wording

“Het Gerechtshof heeft een beslissing genomen naar aanleiding van uw hoger beroep tegen het verbod van verdere publicatie/verspreiding van uw boek. In zijn arrest heeft het Gerechtshof de bezwaren van mevrouw Overduyn tegen verdere publicatie/verspreiding ongegrond verklaard. Het Gerechtshof doet verder geen uitspraken over het rapport 1999/507 van de Nationale ombudsman of dat mevrouw Overduyn wel toegang had tot dossiers bij de MID. Ook in de Memorie van Antwoord van de advocaat van mevrouw Overduyn wordt uitdrukkelijk ontkend dat zij toegang had tot de genoemde dossiers. Gelet op het vorenstaande komen wij niet terug op ons rapport over uw klacht.”

Why this document matters

In this letter of 7 March 2017 the National Ombudsman, Reinier van Zutphen, refuses to withdraw report 1999/507. Giltay had asked him to do so after the 2016 judgment of the Court of Appeal of The Hague. The letter summarises Giltay’s request as follows: the statement of defence of Overduyn’s counsel showed that Overduyn had given the Ombudsman an incorrect account. That filing leaves unchallenged that Giltay had refuted her claim that he had been dismissed as a temp at Casema (point 36). It is precisely this claim that appears in the report as the position of the Minister of Defence.

The letter does not address the dismissal claim or its refutation. From the court document the Ombudsman cites only that, according to her counsel, Overduyn had no access to personnel files. An internal note of 18 May 2017 calls the reply a standard letter. In December 2024, when Van Zutphen was a candidate for the office of European Ombudsman, Giltay wrote to MEPs that Van Zutphen had neither withdrawn nor corrected the report. This is the letter in which Van Zutphen refused to do so.
