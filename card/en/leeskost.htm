LeesKost review, 30 Jan­u­ary 2017

The passage

“High-ranking officials try to clear their names, but are pilloried by their own bungling. […] If it weren’t such a deadly serious matter, the reader might take Edwin’s account for a masterful joke. Especially given the way the undercover agents whom Defence parked on the shop floor at Casema tumble from one beginner’s blunder into the next, so dreadfully amateurish and so far from professional. Even the most average Boy Scout troop would probably handle it better.” The opening sentence of the review and its fourth paragraph. Translated from the Dutch; the original wording is below.

Original wording

“Hooggeplaatsten proberen hun straatje schoon te vegen, maar worden door eigen geklungel aan de schandpaal genageld. […] Als het geen bloed ernstige zaak was zou de lezer het relaas van Edwin kunnen opvatten als een magistrale grap. Zeker zoals de undercoveragenten die door defensie op de werkvloer bij Casema zijn gestald, zo vreselijk amateuristisch en weinig professioneel van de ene beginnersfout in de andere vervallen. De eerste de beste padvindersclub zou het vermoedelijk beter aanpakken.” The review writes ‘bloedernstige’ as two words, ‘bloed ernstige’, and that spelling is kept here.

Why this document matters

LeesKost is one of the publications listed in the essay as having covered the book. This review discusses the second edition, the one that could be printed after the appeal court quashed the ban on De doof­pot­ge­ne­raal, and it reads the affair from the reader’s side rather than the legal one. Ter Steege’s verdict is that high-ranking officials are pilloried by their own bungling, and that the undercover agents are so amateurish that a Scout troop would probably handle it better.
