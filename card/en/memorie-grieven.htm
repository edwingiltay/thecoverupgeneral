Mothers of Sre­bre­nica, statement of appeal, 7 July 2015

The essay cites two passages from this statement of appeal, the Voices section only the second. Both are set out below in the order of the document.

Paragraph 18 · The appearance that the State has destroyed or is withholding the photos

“The appellants will explain that in this case it was for the State to produce the relevant underlying documents in the proceedings under Article 21 of the Code of Civil Procedure, and not to rely on the account given by the NIOD, quite apart from the fact that it at the very least has the appearance that the State, through the intervention of the military intelligence and security service, deliberately destroyed or is withholding the photos in question from the notorious film roll.” Page 14, the penultimate sentence of paragraph 18, on the documents that, according to the appellants, the State should have produced in the proceedings. Translated from the Dutch; the original wording is below.

“Appellanten zullen toelichten dat het in deze zaak op de weg van de Staat had gelegen om de relevante, onderliggende stukken op voet van artikel 21 Rv in het geding te brengen en niet te volstaan met de weergave door het NIOD, nog daargelaten dat het er minstgenomen de schijn van heeft dat de Staat doelbewust door tussenkomst van de militaire inlichtingen- en veiligheidsdienst de bewuste foto’s van het beruchte fotorolletje heeft vernietigd of achterhoudt.” Original wording

Paragraph 31 · The book as a source on the film roll

“There are indications that Rutten’s film roll was deliberately destroyed, or else that the photos in question were developed normally and the story was put about that there had been a failure. A recently published book quotes a female employee of the Military Intelligence Service (MID) who in 1998 complained about the cover-up culture at the MID and mentioned that Rutten’s film roll had not failed and that the photos have been withheld ever since.” Page 23, ground of appeal 1, paragraph 31. Footnote 42 to this paragraph reads: ‘De Doofpot-Generaal, E. Giltay, SpeakEasy Publisher, 2014, p. 29, 134, 191, 192.’ The filing spells the title with a hyphen and a capital G. Translated from the Dutch; the original wording is below.

“Er zijn aanwijzingen dat het betreffende rolletje van Rutten opzettelijk is vernietigd dan wel dat de betreffende foto’s normaal zijn ontwikkeld en het verhaal de wereld in is geholpen dat sprake zou zijn geweest van een mislukking. In een recent verschenen boek wordt een medewerkster van de MID geciteerd die in 1998 haar beklag deed over de doofpotcultuur bij de MID en er gewag van maakte dat het fotorolletje van Rutten niet was mislukt en dat de foto’s sindsdien worden achtergehouden.” Original wording

Why this document matters

In July 2015 the Mothers of Srebrenica cited The Cover-up General in their appeal against the Dutch State, in the passage on the destruction of photographic and video material. Their lawyers referred to four pages of the first edition of the book for the statement by MID employee Barbara Overduyn, quoted there, that Rutten’s film roll had not failed and that the photos have been withheld ever since. They put it forward as an indication that Rutten’s film roll was deliberately destroyed, or that the photos were developed normally and the story of a failure was put about.
