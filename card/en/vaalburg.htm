Karin Vaal­burg, Na­tion­al Om­buds­man, to Giltay, emails, 8 January to 21 Feb­ru­ary 2024

The quotation on the website joins two messages from the same adviser: her verdict on the book from her email of 9 January 2024, and the words ‘with great pleasure’ from her email of 21 February 2024. Both passages are set out below, in the order in which they were written.

Her message of 9 January 2024

“I did indeed read your book (the PDF you sent me, thank you again for that!) in full during my Christmas leave. My com­pli­ments on the very extensive un­der­ly­ing doc­u­men­ta­tion and level of detail in the story! All being well, you will now receive the documents you asked for (from your own file) within 2 weeks. Perhaps we could then make an ap­point­ment to discuss your file. How would you feel about that?” Page 4, the message of 9 January 2024, 14:11, quoted further down the thread. The passage forms one paragraph of the message, which is signed ‘Karin Vaalburg, Senior juridisch adviseur’, senior legal adviser. The remark in brackets refers to the PDF of the book that Giltay had sent her himself. The last two sentences run on as a single sentence in Dutch, joined by a comma; the translation splits them. Translated from the Dutch; the original wording is below.

“Uw boek (de pdf die u mij heeft gestuurd, nogmaals dank daarvoor!) heb ik inderdaad helemaal gelezen tijdens het kerstverlof. Mijn com­pli­men­ten voor de zeer uitgebreide ach­ter­lig­gen­de do­cu­men­ta­tie en ge­de­tail­leerd­heid van het verhaal! Als het goed is ontvangt u nu binnen 2 weken de door u op­ge­vraag­de documenten (van uw eigen dossier). Wellicht dat wij daarna een afspraak kunnen maken om uw dossier te bespreken, hoe staat u daar tegenover?” Original wording

Her message of 21 February 2024

“Incidentally, I wish you every success with the launch of the English-language edition of your book, which I read with great pleasure.” Page 1, the message of 21 February 2024, 18:22, at the top of the thread, signed by Karin Vaalburg. The sentence closes the paragraph about Giltay’s email of 14 February. Translated from the Dutch; the original wording is below.

“Ik wens u overigens bijzonder veel succes met de lancering van de Engelstalige uitgave van uw boek, dat ik met veel plezier heb gelezen.” Original wording

Why this document matters

The interview reports in the 1999 Min­is­try of De­fence document were incorporated word for word, as the Min­is­ter’s official position, into a public Om­buds­man report that is still online today. A quarter of a century later the in­sti­tu­tion’s own senior legal adviser, who according to the essay represented the Om­buds­man against Giltay in related court cases, read The Cover-up Gen­eral over her Christmas leave and praised the book’s documentation in her work cor­re­spon­dence. The same thread shows what followed: she had offered to discuss Giltay’s file, he proposed 27 February 2024 for the meeting in The Hague, and on 12 February she wrote that she had to forgo a follow-up meeting for the time being.
