Marco Ger­rit­sen to Giltay, 24 No­vem­ber 2014

The passage

“We take a keen interest in the subject of your book. The ‘failure’ of the notorious film roll has always been im­plau­si­ble to us. I shall therefore read your book with great interest. Un­for­tu­nate­ly I am unable to attend this coming Wed­nes­day. My colleague Simon van der Sluijs, with whom I am con­duct­ing the case of the 6,000 Moth­ers of Sre­bre­ni­ca against the Dutch State, will be glad to attend. May I register him?” Page 1, the two paragraphs following the line of thanks for the invitation. Translated from the Dutch; the original wording is below.

Original wording

“Het on­der­werp van uw boek kan rekenen op onze warme be­lang­stel­ling. Het ‘mislukken’ van het beruchte fo­to­rol­le­tje is voor ons altijd on­ge­loof­waar­dig geweest. Ik zal uw boek dan ook met grote interesse lezen. Helaas ben ik woensdag aanstaande verhinderd. Mijn collega Simon van der Sluijs, met wie ik de rechtszaak van de 6.000 Moeders van Sre­bre­ni­ca tegen de Nederlandse Staat behandel, zal graag aanwezig zijn. Mag ik hem bij deze aanmelden?” The passage consists of two consecutive paragraphs of the message. The quotation marks around ‘mislukken’, the failure, are the writer’s own; the email sets them as double marks, which appear here as single ones inside the quotation.

Why this document matters

In July 2015 the Moth­ers of Sre­bre­ni­ca cited The Cover-up General in their statement of appeal against the Dutch State. This email shows how early their lawyers took an interest in the book. Marco Ger­rit­sen conducted the case of the 6,000 Moth­ers against the State together with Simon van der Sluijs. Two days before the launch of the first edition, he replied that the subject was of keen interest to them, and that the failure of the notorious film roll had always seemed im­plau­si­ble to them. He was unable to attend himself and registered Van der Sluijs for the launch.
