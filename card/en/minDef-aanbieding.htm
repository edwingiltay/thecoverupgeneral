Giltay to the Minister of Defence, 10 March 2014

The passage

“Your Ex­cel­len­cy, I should be glad to receive your re­sponse to my book. If you send it to me within two weeks, my pub­lish­er Coen Borgman can still include it in full in the forth­com­ing book. Should your re­sponse take a little longer, it can be placed on the book’s website.” Page 1, the third paragraph of the letter. Translated from the Dutch; the original wording is below.

Original wording

“Excellentie, graag verneem ik Uw reactie op mijn boek. Als U mij deze binnen twee weken doet toe­ko­men, kan mijn uit­ge­ver Coen Borgman deze nog in­te­graal opnemen in het te ver­schij­nen boek. Laat Uw reactie iets langer op zich wachten, dan kan deze worden ge­plaatst op de website van het boek.”

Why this document matters

With this letter, handed in at the Min­is­try in person on 10 March 2014, the Min­is­ter of Defence was given the man­u­script of The Cover-up General, with a request for a re­sponse, which could still be included in the book if it arrived within two weeks. The second page is the ac­knowl­edge­ment of receipt, signed at the Min­is­try on the same day. The essay states that in March 2014 the Min­is­ters of Defence and of the In­te­ri­or re­ceived the full man­u­script ahead of pub­li­ca­tion, to­geth­er with a formal no­ti­fi­ca­tion and a dead­line for a re­sponse. That the man­u­script was sent to both min­is­ters with a request to respond has stood, since 2016, as an un­dis­put­ed fact in the appeal judg­ment.
