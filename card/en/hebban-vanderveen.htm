Dick van der Veen, five-star review, Hebban, 23 No­vem­ber 2016

The passage

“Author Edwin Giltay describes in detail how badly things stand with our intelligence services, covered up by Defence, the National Ombudsman, and with damaged ministers in their wake. Like the readers, they will have rolled their eyes in disbelief. A lack of correct data does not release them from the duty of serious truth-finding.” Opening paragraph of the review, sentences three to five. Translated from the Dutch; the original wording is below.

Original wording

“Auteur Edwin Giltay beschrijft tot in details hoe mis het is met onze inlichtingendiensten, toegedekt door Defensie, de Nationale Ombudsman en met beschadigde ministers in het verlengde. Die zullen net als de lezers hun ogen vol ongeloof hebben laten rollen. Het niet voorzien zijn van juiste gegevens ontslaat hen niet van de plicht om serieuze waarheidsvinding te betrachten.” Hebban sets the name Edwin Giltay in bold in the first of these sentences, and spells the reviewer’s name as ‘Dick Van der Veen’, with a capital V. The identical wording appears in the schema.org review data embedded in the page.

Why this document matters

In this review, posted on Hebban on 23 November 2016, Dick van der Veen discusses the second, thor­ough­ly revised edition and closes on the eight new chapters, which deal mainly with the ban on pub­li­ca­tion that was argued for in vain. The essay links to the review where it mentions that addition. Van der Veen reads the affair as the essay does, as a cover-up running from Defence through the National Om­buds­man to damaged ministers, and he holds that not being supplied with the correct data does not release them from the duty to seek the truth in earnest.
