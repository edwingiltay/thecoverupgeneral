Ministry of Defence to Giltay, 30 October 2000

The passage

“Further to your letter of 15 October 2000 I inform you that its content gives me no cause for any further response. In that connection I refer you to my letter of 26 September 2000. To the content of that last letter I have nothing to add. In view of the above I therefore kindly ask you to cease your correspondence, now that the National Ombudsman has found your complaints in this matter unfounded. No further letters from you will be answered.” Page 1, the two paragraphs that make up the body of the letter. Translated from the Dutch; the original wording is below.

Original wording

“Naar aanleiding van uw brief van 15 oktober 2000 deel ik u mede, dat de inhoud daarvan mij geen aanleiding geeft tot een nadere reactie. In dat verband verwijs ik u naar mijn brief van 26 september 2000. Aan de inhoud van laatstgenoemde brief heb ik niets toe te voegen. Gelet op het bovenstaande verzoek ik u dan ook vriendelijk uw correspondentie te staken, nu de Nationale ombudsman uw klachten inzake deze kwestie ongegrond achtte. Op verdere brieven uwerzijds zal niet meer worden gereageerd.” The two paragraphs follow one another directly and are joined here by a single space. The letter writes ‘Nationale ombudsman’ with a lower-case o, as the office itself does.

Why this document matters

In this letter of 30 October 2000, fourteen years before the book appeared, the Ministry of Defence told Giltay that his letter gave it no cause for any further response, asked him to cease his correspondence, and closed with the line that no further letters from him would be answered. Its stated reason was that the National Ombudsman had found his complaints in this matter unfounded. The essay states that the ministries named in the book have never responded substantively to its revelations, at any level, and that this began even before publication, with this letter as the earliest instance.
