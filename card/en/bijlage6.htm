Randstad Callflex reference for Giltay, 20 May 1999

The passage

“We, Randstad Callflex, hereby declare that Mr E.F. Giltay worked for us as a telephone salesperson in the period from 8 June 1998 to 27 July 1998. During that period he performed his duties to the full satisfaction of the client. We wish Mr Giltay every success in his future career.” Page 1, the body of the letter. Translated from the Dutch; the original wording is below.

Original wording

“Hierbij verklaren wij, Randstad Callflex, dat Dhr. E.F. Giltay gedurende de periode van 8 juni 1998 tot 27 juli 1998 voor ons werkzaam is geweest als telefonische verkoper. Hij heeft gedurende deze periode zijn werkzaamheden naar volle tevredenheid van de opdrachtgever verricht. Wij wensen de heer Giltay veel succes in zijn verdere loopbaan.” The letter does not name the client. It speaks only of ‘de opdrachtgever’.

Why this document matters

The characterisation of Giltay by the Minister of Defence is still online today, in the National Ombudsman’s public report of 1999. That report states that Giltay had supposedly been dismissed by Casema after three days for “irritating and maladjusted behaviour”. The essay counters this with considerable evidence to the contrary, including professional references from the companies he had worked for. This letter is one of those references. The temping agency that employed him in the summer of 1998 states in writing that he did his work to the full satisfaction of the client. His employer was therefore Randstad, not Casema, where he worked as an agency worker. Casema could not have dismissed him, contrary to what the Minister claims, nor was he sent away after three days: he worked there for more than six weeks, until the end of July 1998.
