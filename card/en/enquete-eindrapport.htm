Report of the Par­lia­men­tary Committee of Inquiry on Sre­bre­ni­ca, 27 January 2003

The passage

“On 10 April 2002 the long-awaited report of the NIOD on the fall of the Srebrenica enclave appears. Following the NIOD report, the second Kok cabinet tenders its resignation on 16 April 2002.” Page 7, the opening paragraph of section 1.1.2, on the background to the inquiry. Translated from the Dutch; the original wording is below.

Original wording

“Op 10 april 2002 verschijnt het langverwachte rapport van het NIOD over de val van de enclave Srebrenica. Naar aanleiding van het NIOD-rapport biedt het tweede kabinet-Kok op 16 april 2002 zijn ontslag aan.” The paragraph is printed over three lines and the line breaks have been closed up. A superscript footnote marker appears directly after the first sentence, referring to the NIOD report; it has been left out of the quotation.

Why this document matters

This is the parliamentary inquiry’s own account of how heavily Srebrenica weighed politically. Six days after the long-awaited NIOD report appeared, the whole of the second Kok cabinet tendered its resignation. The House of Representatives went on to set up this inquiry, so that it could reach a definitive political judgment on the conduct of the government, the House itself and those responsible in the civil service and the armed forces. The rolls of film belong to that same dossier.
