Deloitte appraisal of Giltay, 6 April 2000

Working with colleagues

“Edwin has fitted in very well to a team comprising 14 different nationalities and his friendly, polite and professional manner has won him many friends. He also speaks good English.” Page 1, the paragraph above the closing paragraph. The document is in English, so the wording is quoted as it is printed on the page. The scan clips the right edge of the frame, so the last letter of ‘polite’ is cut off at the end of the first line; the word is given here in full.

The recommendation

“Edwin has proved to be a vital part of the project team, without whom the project office could not have functioned as efficiently or effectively as it has and I would have no hesitation in recommending him for a similar position.” Page 1, the closing paragraph of the form. The document is in English, so the wording is quoted as it is printed on the page. The scan clips the right edge of the frame, so ‘not’ is cut off after ‘no’ on the first line of that paragraph; the word is given here in full.

Why this document matters

In this feedback form of 6 April 2000 Giltay’s manager at Deloitte Consulting writes that he would have no hesitation in recommending him for a similar position. The essay lists professional references from multinationals, among them IBM and Deloitte, as part of the considerable contradictory evidence against the characterisation of Giltay that the Minister of Defence put on record in 1999 and never withdrew. That characterisation is still online today in the National Ombudsman’s public report.

The manager also writes that the work was always of the highest quality and delivered on time, and that Giltay fitted in well with a team of 14 nationalities, in which his friendly, polite and professional manner won him many friends. The form dates from a year after the characterisation had been set down in black and white.
