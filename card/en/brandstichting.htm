Haag­landen Police, record of arson complaint, 4 January 2011

The passage

“He filed a complaint of arson on his own behalf and on behalf of the injured party […] On Saturday 1 January 2011, at about 02:00, I heard the fire brigade arrive. A little later I heard from a neighbour that there was a fire in my storage unit. On Saturday 1 January 2011, at about 03:00, I saw that various goods in my storage unit had burned. I saw that the lock of the storage unit door had burned. I also saw that the light, the switch and the walls of the storage unit had burned. The storage unit contained various goods belonging to me, which have burned.” Page 1, the line in which the police record the complaint as arson, and page 2, from the complainant’s statement. The intervening text is left out: the details of the injured party and the opening of the statement. Translated from the Dutch; the original wording is below.

Original wording

“Hij deed aangifte terzake brandstichting namens zichzelf en de benadeelde […] Op zaterdag 1 januari 2011, omstreeks 02:00 uur hoorde ik de brandweer aan komen rijden. Even later hoorde ik van een buurman dat er brand in mijn berging was. Op zaterdag 1 januari 2011, omstreeks 03:00 uur zag ik dat er diverse goederen in mijn berging verbrand waren. Ik zag dat het slot van de deur van de berging verbrand was. Ook zag ik dat het lampje, de schakelaar en de wanden van de berging verbrand waren. In de berging stonden diverse goederen die mijn eigendom zijn, die verbrand zijn.”

Why this document matters

On 20 November 2010 Giltay submitted his witness report to three members of the House of Representatives, the parliamentary group leaders Job Cohen, Stef Blok and Geert Wilders, who sat on the Committee on the Intelligence and Security Services. Six weeks later, on the night of 31 December 2010, fire broke out in his storage unit in The Hague. This is the record Haaglanden Police made of the complaint he filed on 4 January 2011, which was registered as arson. Among the burned goods it lists six plastic crates of papers and books. According to the book (p. 127), originals from his dossier were lost in the fire and the perpetrator was never traced. The book considers a link with this affair unlikely but does not rule it out.
