Giltay to the Minister of the Interior, 18 March 2014

The passage

“Excellency, last week I delivered my manuscript to the Minister of Defence with a request for a response. Since not only Defence but also the Ministry of the Interior is involved in the affair I describe, it seems proper to me to send this manuscript to you as well, in keeping with the principle of hearing both sides. I should be glad to receive your response to my book. If you let me have it within two weeks, my publisher Coen Borgman can still include it in full in the forthcoming book. Should your response take a little longer, it can be placed on the book’s website.” Page 1, third and fourth paragraphs. Translated from the Dutch; the original wording is below.

Original wording

“Excellentie, vorige week heb ik mijn manuscript reeds afgegeven aan de minister van Defensie met een verzoek om een reactie. Omdat naast Defensie echter ook Binnenlandse Zaken is betrokken bij de door mij beschreven affaire, lijkt het me correct om in het kader van hoor- en wederhoor dit manuscript ook aan U te zenden. Graag verneem ik Uw reactie op mijn boek. Als U mij deze binnen twee weken doet toekomen, kan mijn uitgever Coen Borgman deze nog integraal opnemen in het te verschijnen boek. Laat Uw reactie iets langer op zich wachten, dan kan deze worden geplaatst op de website van het boek.”

Why this document matters

This is the formal notification the essay refers to. The manuscript of The Cover-up General went to the Minister of the Interior ahead of publication, with a request for a response, which could still be included in the book if it arrived within two weeks and could otherwise be placed on the book’s website. The letter also records that the Minister of Defence had been given the manuscript the week before, so both ministers were offered the chance to reply. The second page is the acknowledgement of receipt, signed at the Ministry on 18 March 2014. That the manuscript was sent to both ministers with a request to respond has stood since 2016 as an undisputed fact in the appeal judgment.
