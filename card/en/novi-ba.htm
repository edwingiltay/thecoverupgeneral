Novi.ba on the cen­sor­ship being lifted, 12 April 2016

The passage

“Former Dutch colonel Charlef Brantz comments: ‘Spies are there to distort reality, they manipulate the truth.’ That is exactly what happened, Mr Giltay points out: ‘It is no wonder that the judges of the Court of Appeal rejected the absurd decision to ban the book.’” Fifth paragraph of the body text, below the standfirst and second from the end. Translated from the Bosnian; the original wording is below. The article renders freely what Brantz said in Dutch. The Dutch wording he approved by email on 29 July 2015 for use by Giltay reads: ‘Spionnen zijn blijkbaar gewend om een loopje met de realiteit te nemen en die zodanig te masseren dat de realiteit beantwoordt aan hun persoonlijke waarheid’ (spies are apparently accustomed to taking liberties with reality and massaging it until it matches their personal truth).

Original wording

“Nekadašnji holandski pukovnik Charlef Brantz komentira: ‘Špijuni su tu da iskrive realnost, oni manipulišu istinom.’ Upravo to se desilo, ističe g. Giltay: ‘Nije čudo što su sudije Apelacionog suda odbacile apsurdnu odluku o zabrani knjige.’” Quoted from the article as published on novi.ba. The source sets the inner quotations with straight apostrophes; they are shown here as typographic single quotation marks.

Why this document matters

On the evening of the judgment, 12 April 2016, this Sarajevo news portal told readers in Bosnia and Herzegovina that the Court of Appeal of The Hague had lifted the ban on the sale of De doof­pot­ge­ne­raal. The article also carries the comment by Colonel (retd) Charlef Brantz on how spies deal with reality. The Voices section quotes Brantz not from the article’s free rendering but from his own Dutch wording, which he approved by email on 29 July 2015. The essay counts the report among the more than four hundred publications through which the attempt to ban the book propelled the story around the world.
