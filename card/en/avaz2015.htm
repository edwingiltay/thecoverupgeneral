Cas­par ten Dam in Dnev­ni Avaz, 4 Jan­u­ary 2016

The Voices section cites this article twice: for the ban as the paper describes it, and for the statement by Caspar ten Dam. Both passages are set out below in the order in which they appear in the article.

The ban as the paper describes it

“Edwin Giltay, the author of the book, as he himself admitted to us, neatly files away all the newspaper articles and is grateful for the support, but the Dutch court has explicitly forbidden him any further form of cooperation with the media, as well as the sale and promotion of the book, which is unprecedented in the recent history of the land of tulips.” Second paragraph of the article. Trans­lat­ed from the Bos­ni­an; the original wording is below.

“Edwin Giltay, autor knjige, kako nam je sam priznao, uredno slaže sve novinske napise i zahvaljuje na podršci, ali bilo kakav dalji oblik saradnje s medijima holandski sud mu je izričito zabranio, kao i prodaju i promociju knjige, što je nezabilježeno u novijoj historiji zemlje tulipana.” Original wording

Caspar ten Dam on the judgment

“‘The author expected a judgment in his favour, because to ban a thoroughly researched and documented book is beyond belief! Before it even went to press, Giltay sent the manuscript of the book to the Dutch Minister of Defence, who did not oppose publication,’ Ten Dam explained, adding that Giltay will appeal against the judgment and, if need be, will pursue this case with the Amsterdam law firm Boekx all the way to the European Court.” Fifth paragraph of the article, the second of the two statements by Caspar ten Dam. Trans­lat­ed from the Bos­ni­an; the original wording is below. Quoted from the article as published on avaz.ba. The paper prints Ten Dam’s words in plain double quotation marks; they are set here in single quotation marks inside the outer quotation. The headline spells the first name Casper, while the body of the article spells it Caspar.

“‘Autor je očekivao presudu u svoju korist jer zabraniti jednu temeljito istraženu i dokumentovanu knjigu je za nepovjerovati! Prije samog štampanja Giltay je poslao rukopis knjige holandskom ministru odbrane, koji se nije usprotivio publikaciji’, pojasnio je Ten Dam uz napomenu da će Giltay uložiti žalbu na presudu i ako bude potrebno, sa amsterdamskom advokatskom kompanijom Boekx tjerati ovaj slučaj sve do Evropskog suda.” Original wording

Why this document matters

On 4 January 2016 Dnevni Avaz reports that the Hague ban reached beyond sale and promotion to any further press contact, so that Giltay’s friend Caspar ten Dam spoke to the media in his place. The essay lists this article among the coverage of the total ban that the District Court of The Hague imposed on 14 December 2015. Ten Dam calls the banning of a thoroughly researched and documented book beyond belief, and points out that before printing, Giltay had sent the manuscript to the Dutch Minister of Defence, who did not oppose publication. He adds that Giltay will appeal and, with the Amsterdam law firm Boekx, carry the case as far as the European Court should that prove necessary.
