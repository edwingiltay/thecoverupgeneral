Defence to the National Ombudsman on Giltay, 7 Oc­to­ber 1999

The passage

“In this case […] told Giltay on the afternoon of 11 June 1998, in a short conversation, that because he had too strong a personality he was not considered suitable for the post he was seeking.” Page 1, the last paragraph, where the head of the psychological examination section at Defence Recruitment and Selection describes the rejection interview of 11 June 1998. Translated from the Dutch; the original wording is below.

Original wording

“In dit geval heeft […] aan Giltay op de middag van 11 juni 1998 in een kort gesprekje verteld dat hij ivm zijn te sterke persoonlijkheid niet geschikt werd geacht voor de dor hem geambieerde functie.” The square brackets mark a name blacked out in the document. The Dutch abbreviation ‘ivm’ stands for ‘in verband met’, on account of, and ‘dor hem’ is a typing error for ‘door hem’; both are left as they stand.

Why this document matters

This telephone note of 7 October 1999 records what Defence Recruitment and Selection told the National Ombudsman about the screening of Giltay on 11 June 1998: on the afternoon of that day he was told he was not considered suitable because his personality was too strong. On the second page the head of the psychological examination section explains that ground of rejection by saying that for such posts the Royal Netherlands Navy needs people who are still readily ‘malleable and mouldable’. The essay counts this note as evidence against the characterisation of Giltay that the Minister of Defence put on record and that still stands in the Ombudsman’s public report of 1999. The assessment is Defence’s own; the Ombudsman received it from the Ministry in the same complaint file on which his report rests.
