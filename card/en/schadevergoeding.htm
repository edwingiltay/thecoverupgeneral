Leidsch Dagblad, report on the damages claim, 21 No­vem­ber 2015

The Voices section quotes the lawyer’s words from this report. The opening of the report, which sets out the case, comes first.

The opening of the report

“Former army general Ad van Baal of Al­phen is por­trayed as a sort of mafia boss, his wife as a spy and a thief. Barbara Over­duyn, a former em­ploy­ee of the Military In­tel­li­gence Service, does not come off well either in the con­tro­ver­sial book De Doof­pot­ge­ne­raal. On Friday, in preliminary relief pro­ceed­ings, she is de­mand­ing com­pen­sa­tion from author Edwin Gilthay, plus a rec­ti­fi­ca­tion.” Opening paragraph of the report, page 8, following the dateline Alphen aan den Rijn. The paper spells the author’s surname as “Gilthay”, here and again in the next sentence; it is Giltay. The report is set in narrow columns with hyphenated line breaks, and those breaks have been closed up in the wording above. Translated from the Dutch; the original wording is below. De doof­pot­ge­ne­raal is the book’s original Dutch title; the English edition is The Cover-up Gen­eral.

“Oud-land­macht­ge­ne­raal Ad van Baal uit Al­phen wordt ge­por­tret­teerd als een soort maf­fia­baas, zijn vrouw als een spion en die­veg­ge. Ook Barbara Over­duyn, oud-me­de­werk­ster van de Mi­li­tai­re In­lich­tin­gen­dienst komt er niet best vanaf in het om­stre­den boek De Doof­pot­ge­ne­raal. Vrijdag eist zij in kort geding een scha­de­ver­goe­ding van auteur Edwin Gilthay, plus een rec­ti­fi­ca­tie.” Original wording

What her lawyer says

“‘There isn’t a jot of truth in that book’, says lawyer Ramon Thunnissen. ‘It is full of strange things. […] My client Overduyn slept badly because of it, very unpleasant.’” The paragraph with her lawyer’s reaction, further on in the same report. The sentence about Van Baal’s wife has been left out. In the report the lawyer’s words continue for one more sentence after ‘very unpleasant’. The paper sets the lawyer’s words in low and high double quotation marks; they are set as single ones here, inside the outer quotation. Translated from the Dutch; the original wording is below.

“‘Er klopt geen jota van dat boek’, zegt advocaat Ramon Thunnissen. ‘Het staat vol rare dingen. […] Mijn cliënte Overduyn heeft er slecht van geslapen, heel vervelend.’” Original wording

Why this document matters

On 21 No­vem­ber 2015, days before the hearing, the Leidsch Dagblad reported that a former employee of the Military Intelligence Service (MID) was de­mand­ing com­pen­sa­tion and a rec­ti­fi­ca­tion from the author of The Cover-up General in preliminary relief pro­ceed­ings, and it quoted her lawyer saying there was not a jot of truth in the book. On 14 De­cem­ber 2015 pre­li­mi­na­ry relief judge M.E. Groeneveld-Stubbe banned the book; on 12 April 2016 the Court of Appeal lifted that ban and held that the only undisputed in­ac­cu­ra­cies, the former employee’s age and the spelling of her name, were in­suf­fi­cient to cast doubt on the me­ti­cu­lous­ness with which the author had pro­ceed­ed.
