Trouw, on the technical examination of the film roll, 29 June 2006

The passage

“The blank Fuji colour film of the then Dutchbat lieutenant Rutten has come into the hands of the editors of the tele­vi­sion pro­gramme ‘Nova’, who had it examined by the director of the broad­cast­ing photo company and by Agfa experts in Munich. Both reach the con­clu­sion that the film has been destroyed so thoroughly that this can only have been done de­lib­er­ate­ly.” Page 1, opening of the article. Translated from the Dutch; the original wording is below.

Original wording

“Het blanke Fuji-kleurenfilmpje van toenmalig Dutchbat-luitenant Rutten is in handen gekomen van de redactie van het televisieprogramma ‘Nova’, die het liet onderzoeken door de directeur van het omroepfotobedrijf en Agfa-deskundigen in Munchen. Beiden komen tot de conclusie dat het filmpje zo grondig is vernield, dat dit alleen met opzet gebeurd kan zijn.” The page prints the German city as “Munchen”, without the umlaut. The article breaks off in a later sentence, where the paywall begins.

Why this document matters

In this report of 29 June 2006, Trouw states that according to experts the blank Fuji colour film of the then Dutchbat lieutenant Johannes Rutten had been destroyed so thoroughly that “this can only have been done de­lib­er­ate­ly”. The film had reached the editors of the tele­vi­sion pro­gramme Nova, who had it examined by Meulenman, director of the broad­cast­ing photo company NOB-foto, and by Agfa experts in Munich. Trouw adds that this raises fresh doubt about whether De­fence staff lied about the mistakes made with the ministry’s developing equipment. The essay says the film roll captured Bosnian Serb war crimes and that the Dutch military wanted to obscure that evidence.
