W.J. Deetman, Haag­lan­den police authority, to Giltay, 14 December 2000

The passage

“My judgement on whether your notice of objection is well founded accords with the advice of the committee, and it reads: ‘I declare the notice of objection well founded as regards the failure to keep the promise made by the station chief. I withhold judgement as regards the objection concerning the treatment by the second detective.’” Page 1, the ruling, in the two paragraphs that follow the mention of the independent police complaints committee. Translated from the Dutch; the original wording is below.

Original wording

“Mijn oordeel over de gegrondheid van uw bezwaarschrift komt overeen met het advies van de commissie en luidt: ‘Ik verklaar het bezwaarschrift gegrond voor wat betreft het niet nakomen van de toezegging van de bureauchef. Ik onthoud mij van een oordeel voor wat betreft het bezwaar m.b.t. de bejegening door de tweede rechercheur.’” Line breaks in the scan have been removed. The letter itself puts the inner quotation in double quotation marks and prints “gegrond” and “onthoud mij van een oordeel” in bold; that emphasis is not reproduced here.

Why this document matters

In July 1999 the police refused to record the criminal complaint of slander and libel that Giltay wanted to file against Barbara Overduyn, and he objected to how his grievance about the police’s conduct was then dealt with. After Queen Beatrix referred his letter of 14 October 2000 to the Minister of the Interior, Giltay’s notice of objection of 22 July 2000 was answered by The Hague’s mayor Wim Deetman, who signs here as the authority responsible for the Haaglanden police force.

He declares the objection well founded as regards a promise the station chief did not keep, and withholds judgement on how the second detective treated Giltay. He also states that he has asked for his ruling to be brought to the attention of the officers involved, so that lessons are drawn from it.
