Mohamed Alsiadi on In­sta­gram, 22 April 2025

The passage

“It is beyond doubt that The Cover-up General is an important basis for documenting the genocide in Bosnia. Samantha Power, the American journalist whom Obama later chose to be the American ambassador to the United Nations, was on the ground documenting and reporting on the genocide, but the American Secretary of State Madeleine Albright was denying it, even though the American and Russian security services were closely monitoring the criminal events as they happened, with the details archived in their files.” The whole of the comment by mohamed_alsiadi, the first comment shown under the post. Trans­lat­ed from the Arabic; the original wording is below.

Original wording

“لا شك بان الكتاب اساس مهم لتوثيق الابادة الجماعية في البوسنة. سامانثا باور الصحفية الامريكية وفيما بعد اختارها أوباما لتكون سفيرة امريكا في الامم المتحدة كانت على الارض توثق وتنشر عن الابادة ولكن وزيرة الخارجية الأمريكية مادلين أولبريت كانت تنفي علماً بان الأجهزة الأمنية الأمريكية والروسية كانت تراقب عن كثب الأحداث الاجرامية بوقتها وبتفاصيل مؤرشفة لديها.” The comment is written in Arabic and runs from right to left. It spells Albright أولبريت, a variant of the usual أولبرايت. The comment says only ‘the book’; the post it answers is about The Cover-up General.

Why this document matters

Al Jazeera Documentary told its Arabic-speaking audience, in an Instagram post, the story of the book that was banned in the Netherlands, and this comment is one of the responses to that post. Prof. Mohamed Alsiadi, a genocide and human rights expert at Rutgers University in the United States, writes that the book is an important basis for documenting the genocide in Bosnia.
