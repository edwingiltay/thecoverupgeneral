Exhibit · Criminal complaint

Criminal complaint filed with The Hague police, 12 June 2000

The passage

“She was remarkably candid about many things, including her daily work, and volunteered a great deal of detail, explaining to me unprompted and in detail, among other things, the failure of the photographs taken in connection with the fall of Srebrenica. Unprompted, she stated that the failure of the so-called failed development of the photographs taken was a fabrication, and that she had seen the photos herself.” Page 1, from the paragraph under the heading ‘Kennismaking met mevrouw Barbara Overduin’. The double wording, ‘the failure of the so-called failed development’, reflects her account: according to her, the development of the photographs had not failed. Translated from the Dutch; the original wording is below.

Original wording

“Zij was opmerkelijk openhartig over vele zaken; ook betreffende haar dagelijkse werk en vertelde volop details waarbij zij onder meer het mislukken van de gemaakte foto’s met betrekking tot de val van Srebrenica in detail aan mij ongevraagd heeft uitgelegd. Ongevraagd verklaarde zij dat het mislukken van de zogenaamd mislukte ontwikkeling van de gemaakte foto’s een fabel was en dat zij zelf de foto’s had gezien.” The complaint spells the name “Barbara Overduin”, as does the first edition of the book; later editions anonymise her name, and the essay uses “Overduyn”. Words broken across lines in the original, such as ‘Onge-’ and ‘vraagd’, are closed up here.

Why this document matters

The Hague detectives had refused to record Giltay’s criminal complaint in July 1999. Only after district chief N. Laagland upheld Giltay’s grievance on two points and offered his apologies in March 2000 was the complaint of slander and libel finally recorded, on 12 June 2000. Giltay used it to put on record what the Military Intelligence Service employee had told him about the photographic material: that the failure of the development was a fabrication, and that she had seen the photos herself.

According to the letter of 19 September 2000 closing the case (reference PL1523/1999/16079), on the letterhead of the Haaglanden police and signed by the public prosecutor, the investigation produced no result and the complaint was closed with no further action (‘opgelegd’).
