Public hearing of De Ruyter, Par­lia­men­tary Committee of Inquiry on Sre­bre­ni­ca, 6 December 2002

The passage

“I think that the whole course of events around the roll of film was an accumulation of situations in which Murphy’s law struck. It can never be put right, it is very sad, but no more than that. It is just bad luck, again a great deal of bad luck.” Page 701, De Ruyter’s closing statement, in answer to the chair’s final question whether there was anything the committee had not asked him about. Translated from the Dutch; the original wording is below.

Original wording

“Ik denk dat de hele gang van zaken rond het fotorolletje een opeenstapeling is geweest van situaties waarin de wet van Murphy toesloeg. Het is nooit meer te herstellen, het is heel erg triest, maar méér niet. Het is gewoon pech, nogmaals heel veel pech geweest.” The transcript is set in narrow columns; the line breaks inside the passage have been closed up. The double accent in “méér” is as printed.

Why this document matters

Counter-intelligence Major De Ruyter took receipt of Rutten’s roll of film, and here he accounts for its fate under oath. He was a major in MID/KL, the Army branch of the Military Intelligence Service, and so belonged to the Army, as did Dutchbat. In his closing words he sums it up as an accumulation of situations in which Murphy’s law struck, in short “just bad luck”. Four years earlier General Couzy had already given his own account: according to him, the MID had wanted to develop the roll in-house. The same major conducted the February 1999 questioning of Overduyn from which the ministerial position emerged that Giltay was “completely insane”.
