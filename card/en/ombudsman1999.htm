Na­tion­al Om­buds­man report 1999/507, 17 De­cem­ber 1999

The essay refers to the statement about the dismissal on page 12, the Voices section to the characterisation ‘completely insane’ on page 13. Both are in the same interview report.

The statement about the dismissal

“That for three days in the month of June, from 18.00 to 22.00 hours, she worked together with (the complainant; N.o.) at Q in The Hague. After those three days (the complainant; N.o.) was supposedly dismissed for ‘irritating and maladjusted behaviour’.” Page 12, Section C.5, the first statement in the report of the interview of 11 February 1999 with the former MID employee, after the opening ‘Y verklaarde:’ (Y stated). The statement is set as a bullet point; the bullet is left out here. In the report’s anonymisation, ‘Q’ stands for the cable company Casema. Translated from the Dutch; the original wording is below.

“In de maand juni gedurende drie dagen, van 18.00 tot 22.00 uur, met (verzoeker; N.o.) te hebben samengewerkt bij Q te Den Haag. Na deze drie dagen zou (verzoeker; N.o.) zijn ontslagen wegens ‘irritant en onaangepast gedrag’.” Original wording

‘Completely insane’

“She shared the view of her colleagues at Q that (the complainant; N.o.) was ‘completely insane’.” Page 13, Section C.5, from the same interview report, which the Minister of Defence submitted as part of his position. ‘N.o.’ marks an editorial insertion by the National Ombudsman; the report replaces Casema with ‘Q’ and the complainant with ‘verzoeker’. The bullet is left out. Translated from the Dutch; the original wording is below.

“Zij de mening van haar Q collegae deelde dat (verzoeker; N.o.) ‘volledig gestoord’ was.” Original wording

Why this document matters

Giltay turned to the National Ombudsman in December 1998 with a complaint about his rejection by the Royal Netherlands Navy and about the part played by the Military Intelligence Service. The Minister of Defence answered with a position that the Ombudsman reproduces in this public report, including the report of an interview with former MID employee Barbara Overduyn. She stated that Giltay had supposedly been dismissed from the cable company Casema, where he worked as a temporary worker through the agency Randstad Callflex, for “irritating and maladjusted” behaviour, and that she shared her colleagues’ view that he was “completely insane”. The essay notes that this characterisation remains online today, in the Ombudsman’s public report of 1999, despite considerable evidence to the contrary such as the Defence assessment of 1998 that attested to a strong character.

Report 1999/507 of the National Ombudsman, 18 pages
Date
17 December 1999
Complaint
Request received on 2 December 1998; the report anonymises the complainant as ‘de heer X’ and thereafter as ‘verzoeker’
Subject
The handling of an application for the post of officer with the Royal Netherlands Navy, and the claims that Defence Recruitment and Selection had wrongly cited drug use as a ground for rejection and that the Military Intelligence Service had brought incriminating information into the selection procedure at an early stage
Passage
Section C.5, the Minister of Defence’s position, pages 12 and 13
Source of the passage
Report of an interview held on 11 February 1999 with the former MID employee, submitted by the Minister as part of his position
Mandate
The position was set out on the Minister’s behalf by Secretary-General Barth, by letter of 2 March 1999, and counts as that of the Minister of Defence, Frank de Grave, who bears the responsibility for it. The quotation in the Voices section is therefore attributed to him.
Findings
Both parts of the complaint were declared not well-founded; on both counts the conduct investigated was found proper

1998.07789, taken from the published file name; the report itself does not state it
