De Volkskrant, books column, 22 Oc­to­ber 2016

The passage

“About the failed roll of film from Sre­bre­ni­ca, and about the tangle of intrigues and smoke­screens sur­round­ing the dis­ap­pear­ance of this potential evidence of war crimes, Edwin Giltay wrote De doof­pot­ge­ne­raal in 2014. After a ‘defence spy’ brought preliminary relief pro­ceed­ings, he was placed under a speaking and pub­li­ca­tion ban. That was quashed on appeal, so that an expanded second edition is now appearing, with an after­word by Hans Laroes and endorsements from, among others, Harry van Bommel and Jan Pronk.” The complete book notice. The paper puts ‘defensiespionne’ (the feminine form of ‘defence spy’) between its own single quotation marks. Translated from the Dutch; the original wording is below. De doof­pot­ge­ne­raal is the book’s original Dutch title; the English edition is The Cover-up Gen­eral.

Original wording

“Over het mislukte fotorolletje uit Srebrenica, en over de warboel van intriges en rookgordijnen rond het verdwijnen van dit mogelijke bewijsmateriaal van oorlogsmisdaden, schreef Edwin Giltay in 2014 De doof­pot­ge­ne­raal. Nadat een ‘defensiespionne’ een kort geding aanspande, kreeg hij een spreek- en publicatieverbod. In hoger beroep is dat herroepen, zodat nu een uitgebreide tweede druk verschijnt, met een nawoord van Hans Laroes en aanbevelingen van onder anderen Harry van Bommel en Jan Pronk.” The paper writes ‘defensiespionne’, the feminine form, in single quotation marks of its own.

Why this document matters

On 22 October 2016, in a short book notice, de Volks­krant sums up in one sentence what the book is about: the failed roll of film from Sre­bre­ni­ca and the tangle of intrigues around the dis­ap­pear­ance of that potential evidence of war crimes. It then notes that the speaking and pub­li­ca­tion ban was quashed on appeal, so that an expanded second edition could appear, with an after­word by Hans Laroes. The essay mentions de Volks­krant in its timeline under the revised second edition of September 2016, and the Voices section quotes the notice’s first sentence.
