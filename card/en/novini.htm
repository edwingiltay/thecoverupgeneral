Novini, Defensie flatert voort, 25 August 2018

The essay and the Voices section cite this article for three different statements by the Minister. The passages are set out below in the order of the article.

What the Minister said about the book

“Ank Bijleveld states when asked: “In the book, fact and fiction do indeed become intertwined.”” Page 3, second paragraph of the section headed ‘Diskrediet’. The article puts the Minister’s words in double quotation marks, kept here inside the quotation.

“Ank Bijleveld verklaart desgevraagd: “In het boek lopen feiten en fictie inderdaad door elkaar heen.”” Original wording

What the Minister said about the appeal judgment

“On appeal, however, the Court of Appeal of The Hague swept the objections aside and held that there is no doubt about the care taken over the book, which is available again. “Defence has no further opinion on that,” says Bijleveld.” The sentences that follow directly on the passage above, in the same paragraph.

“In hoger beroep veegde het Gerechtshof Den Haag de bezwaren echter van tafel en stelde dat er geen twijfel bestaat over de zorgvuldigheid van het inmiddels weer verkrijgbare boek. “Daar vindt Defensie verder niets van,” zegt Bijleveld.” Original wording

The denial that Defence ever said it

“At the time Giltay was described as “completely insane” in a report officially released by the ministry. In 2018 the Minister denies standing behind that report: “Defence has never spoken of Mr Giltay in this way.” The document has nevertheless never been withdrawn and turns out to be still public.” The next sentences of the same section. The report referred to is the 1999 report by the MID Chief of Staff.

“Giltay werd indertijd in een door het ministerie officieel naar buiten gebracht rapport aangemerkt als “volledig gestoord”. De bewindsvrouw ontkent anno 2018 achter dit rapport te staan: “Defensie heeft zich nooit in deze zin over de heer Giltay uitgelaten.” Het document is desondanks nimmer herroepen en blijkt nog steeds openbaar.” Original wording

Why this document matters

In this article of 25 August 2018 Minister of Defence Ank Bijleveld says: “In the book, fact and fiction do indeed become in­ter­twined.” The essay sets this against the Min­is­ter of Jus­tice, who stated in 2021 that the labels fake news, con­spir­a­cy theories and anti-government prop­a­gan­da were in no way based on the content of The Cover-up General. The same paragraph of the article records that the Court of Ap­peal of The Hague had swept the ob­jec­tions aside and found there was no doubt about the care with which the book was written, after which the article has the Min­is­ter say: “Defence has no further opinion on that.” The Court of Appeal had already ruled on that question in 2016, two years before this statement by the Minister. She then denies that De­fence has ever spoken of Giltay in this way, while the article records that the report calling him completely insane was never withdrawn. The Voices section quotes all three statements.
