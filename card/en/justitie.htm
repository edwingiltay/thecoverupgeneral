Min­is­ter of Jus­tice and Se­cu­ri­ty to Giltay, 12 January 2021

The essay cites this letter for two things. Both are set out below, each with its own link.

Not based on the book

“The classifications mentioned in that connection are based, among other things, on broadcasts and statements on social media from the publishing house and from persons involved with the publishing house, and are in no way based on the content of individual books published by the house, such as the book to which you refer.” Page 1 of the reply, page 2 of the PDF, third paragraph.

“De classificaties die daarbij worden genoemd, zijn onder meer gebaseerd op uitzendingen en uitlatingen op sociale media vanuit de uitgeverij en personen die betrokken zijn bij de uitgeverij, en zijn geenszins gebaseerd op de inhoud van individuele boeken die door de uitgeverij zijn uitgegeven, zoals het boek waaraan u refereert.” Original wording

‘Danger to the State’ does not apply

“The term ‘danger to the State’ that you suggest does not apply to the trend identified.” The closing sentence of the same paragraph. Giltay had used the term himself: in his letter to the NCTV he wrote that he had never known his publisher was a danger to the State.

“De door u gesuggereerde term ‘staatsgevaarlijk’ is niet van toepassing op de gesignaleerde trendmatigheid.” Original wording

Why this document matters

In this letter of 12 January 2021 the Minister of Justice and Security confirms that the passage on the publisher de Blauwe Tijger in Terrorist Threat As­sess­ment Netherlands 53 (DTN53) is not based on Giltay’s book. On 23 November 2020 Giltay had asked the Na­tion­al Co­or­di­na­tor for Coun­ter­ter­ror­ism and Se­cu­ri­ty (NCTV) whether his book too fell under ‘anti-government pro­pa­gan­da’, ‘fake news’ or ‘con­spir­a­cy theories’, and if so, which passages and page numbers.

The answer does not literally say no, but that is what it amounts to: the clas­si­fi­ca­tions are in no way based on the content of the publisher’s individual books, his included, and the answer names no passages or page numbers. The term ‘danger to the State’, which Giltay himself had raised, does not apply to ‘the trend identified’, the letter adds.

The essay contrasts this letter with the Ministry of Defence, which in 2018 described the book as one in which fact and fiction become intertwined.
