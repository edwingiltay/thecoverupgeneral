Exhibit · Tweet

Len­ne­ke Sprik on Twitter, 26 De­cem­ber 2014

The passage

“I’m reading ‘De Doofpotgeneraal’ by Edwin Giltay with bated breath. It feels like fiction, so bizarrely thrilling and worrying at the same time.” The whole of the tweet. Trans­lat­ed from the Dutch; the original wording is below. De doof­pot­ge­ne­raal is the book’s original Dutch title; the English edition is The Cover-up Gen­eral.

Original wording

“Ik lees met ingehouden adem ‘De Doofpotgeneraal’ van Edwin Giltay. Het lijkt wel fictie, zo bizar spannend en zorgwekkend tegelijkertijd.” The tweet encloses the title in straight single quotation marks, which are set here as typographic ones, and it capitalises the title as ‘De Doofpotgeneraal’, whereas the quotation on the website gives De doof­pot­ge­ne­raal. The message carries no link, no image and no hashtag.

Why this document matters

Sprik said in public what the book did to her: she was reading it with bated breath, and it felt like fiction, bizarrely thrilling and worrying at the same time. Seven months later, in the same thread, Gil­tay told her that something no less bizarre had followed, a demand to have his book banned, and she answered that she had read it and was going from one as­ton­ish­ment to the next. Alongside her quotation in the Voices section, she is described as Dr Lenneke Sprik, a lecturer in in­ter­na­tion­al security and an expert in UN peace­keep­ing operations. She is the author of Protection of Civilians and Individual Accountability (Routledge Research in the Law of Armed Conflict, 2019), on the ac­count­abil­i­ty of military commanders in UN peace­keep­ing operations, with Srebrenica as one of its case studies.
