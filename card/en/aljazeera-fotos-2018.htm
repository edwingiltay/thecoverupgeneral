Al Jazeera Balkans interview, 21 July 2018

The passage

“As circumstances would have it, Edwin Giltay found himself at the centre of a scandal in which the military intelligence service sought to conceal the existence of photographs, and partly succeeded. Because the ‘affair’ was exposed, the book was banned, and Giltay himself came under attack from the intelligence services.” Introduction to the interview, third paragraph, before the first question. Translated from the Bosnian; the original wording is below.

Original wording

“Edwin Giltay, sticajem okolnosti, našao se u središtu skandala u kojem je vojno – obavještajna služba nastojala prikriti postojanje fotografija, u čemu je djelimično i uspjela. Zbog otkrivanja „afere“ knjiga je bila zabranjena, a sam Giltay našao se na udaru obavještajnih službi.” The article writes ‘vojno – obavještajna služba’ (military intelligence service) with a spaced dash throughout, and sets the book title as ‘The Cover – up General’ in the preceding paragraph. The low and high quotation marks around „afere“ follow the style of the original.

Why this document matters

Under the headline ‘The Netherlands concealed photographs of dead Bosniaks from Srebrenica’, Al Jazeera Balkans reported on 21 July 2018 that the book was banned and that Giltay came under attack from the intelligence services. In the interview he himself marks the limits of his account: his book describes the espionage scandal inside the Netherlands and offers no first-hand information about the events of 1995. The essay counts the interview among the sustained, largely Bosnian and regional coverage of the affair between 2018 and 2023.
