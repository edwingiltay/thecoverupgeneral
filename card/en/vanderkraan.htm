Vincent van der Kraan, comment, 16 April 2016

The passage

“I had the privilege of reading the manuscript and it made my hair stand on end. I do not know whether the book, which is not yet on sale here in Brazil, matches the manuscript exactly, but I dreaded its publication. ‘This won’t be appreciated,’ I commented, adding: ‘Watch out for repercussions!’ Fortunately Edwin is no longer on his own and has the press behind him, and hopefully the politicians too. I can warmly recommend that a thorough investigation into this cover-up affair still be carried out. I fear there was some serious lying at a high level at the time.” Comment 6 of 7, page 2. Translated from the Dutch; the original wording is below.

Original wording

“Ik heb het manuscript mogen lezen en mijn haren rezen ter berge. Ik weet niet of het boek (nog niet hier in Brazilië te koop) één op één loopt met het manuscript maar ik hield mijn hart vast voor publicatie. Dit wordt je niet in dank afgenomen, was mijn commentaar en: pas op voor repercussies! Gelukkig staat Edwin niet meer alleen en heeft hij de Pers achter zich en hopelijk ook de politiek. Ik kan van harte aanbevelen om alsnog grondig onderzoek naar deze doofpot affaire te verrichten. Ik vrees dat er op hoog niveau flink gelogen is in der tijd.” The closing words ‘in der tijd’ are the writer’s own and are left as they stand.

Why this document matters

Van der Kraan, an editor, read the manuscript of De doof­pot­ge­ne­raal before it was published and told Giltay that the book would not be ap­pre­ci­ated and that he should watch out for re­per­cus­sions. He recounted this in the comments under a weblog post by Harry van Bommel, then an SP member of the House of Rep­re­sen­ta­tives, welcoming the Court of Appeal judgment that had quashed the ban on the book earlier that week. He recommended a thorough in­ves­ti­ga­tion into the affair, and added that he feared there had been some serious lying at a high level at the time.
