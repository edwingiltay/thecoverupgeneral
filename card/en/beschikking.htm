Court of Appeal of The Hague, decision on the Article 12 complaint, 19 December 2002

The passage

“In February 1999 an investigation took place into the involvement of two former staff members of the Military Intelligence Service (MID) in the complainant’s application for a post with the Marine Corps. In the course of this investigation the person complained against was also heard. She works at the MID. According to a report of that interview in the case file, she declared that the complainant had been dismissed from Casema, a company where she had worked alongside him for a few days on a temporary basis, on grounds of ‘irritating and maladjusted behaviour’. She also declared that she shared the opinion of her Casema colleague that the complainant was ‘completely insane’. It was on the basis of these remarks that the complainant asked for her prosecution for slander or libel.” Page 2 of the decision, para. 7. Translated from the Dutch; the original wording is below.

Original wording

“In februari 1999 heeft een onderzoek plaatsgevonden naar de betrokkenheid van twee voormalige personeelsleden van de Militaire Inlichtingen Dienst (MID) bij de sollicitatie van klager naar een functie bij het Korps Mariniers. In het kader van dit onderzoek is ook beklaagde gehoord. Beklaagde is werkzaam bij de MID. Zij verklaarde, blijkens een zich bij de stukken bevindend verslag van dit interview, dat klager wegens “irritant en onaangepast gedrag” was ontslagen bij Casema, een onderneming waar zij samen met klager enkele dagen op uitzendbasis had gewerkt. Ook verklaarde zij dat zij de mening van haar Casema-collega deelde, dat klager “volkomen gestoord” was. Op basis van deze opmerkingen heeft klager het verzoek tot vervolging van beklaagde wegens smaad dan wel laster gedaan.” The decision spells the name of the person complained against as “Overduin”.

Why this document matters

In 2018 Minister of Defence Ank Bijleveld denied that Defence had ever called Giltay “completely insane”. This decision records that an MID employee, according to the interview report of February 1999, had declared that she shared her Casema colleague’s opinion that Giltay was “completely insane”. The report itself uses the plural: her “CASEMA collegae”. The Minister of Defence adopted that report in the position he submitted to the National Ombudsman and so endorsed the characterisation. The court dismissed the complaint not because the statements had not been made, but because it could not be established whether the employee had meant to give them publicity.
