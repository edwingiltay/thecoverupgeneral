State­ment con­cern­ing Bar­bara Over­duin by a Ca­se­ma col­league, 10 June 1999

The passage

“One evening in August 1998, at the time when I was working at Casema, Mrs Barbara Overduin ordered me to stop associating with Mr Edwin Giltay, because she knew terrible things about him. His background was so bad that, according to Mrs Barbara Overduin, Mr Edwin Giltay would not dare to tell me about it.” Page 1, the body of the statement. Translated from the Dutch; the original wording is below. The Dutch runs on as a single sentence without a full stop and is set here as two.

Original wording

“OP EEN AVOND IN AUGUSTUS 1998, IN DE TIJD DAT IK BIJ CASEMA WERKTE GEBOOD MEVROUW BARBARA OVERDUIN MIJ NIET MEER MET DE HEER EDWIN GILTAY OM TE GAAN OMDAT ZE ERGE DINGEN OVER HEM WIST, DE ANTECEDENTEN WAREN ZO SLECHT DAT VOLGENS MEVROUW BARBARA OVERDUIN, DE HEER EDWIN GILTAY DEZE MIJ NIET ZOU DURVEN TE VERTELLEN” The whole page is typed in capitals. ‘Antecedenten’ is the Dutch word for a person’s prior record, the record a screening checks. The witness writes the name as Overduin. Her name was spelled in various ways; officially it was Overduyn.

Why this document matters

In this statement of 10 June 1999 a man who worked at Ca­se­ma puts on record that Bar­bara Over­duin, one evening in August 1998, ordered him to stop associating with Edwin Giltay, because she knew terrible things about Giltay. Giltay’s background was said to be so bad that he would not dare to tell the man about it himself. The witness declares that he makes the statement entirely of his own free will. His name, signature and street address are blacked out in the scan to protect him.
