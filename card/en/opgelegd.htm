Haag­lan­den Police to Giltay, handling of the criminal complaint, 19 September 2000

The passage

“On Monday 12 June 2000 you filed a criminal complaint of slander/libel. An investigation was carried out in response. An official report of it was drawn up, which is registered under number PL1523/1999/ 16079. Having studied the official report, I inform you that the investigation has produced no result and will therefore be ‘shelved’ in the records.” Page 1, the first two paragraphs of the letter. Translated from the Dutch; the original wording is below. ‘Shelved’ renders the Dutch ‘opgelegd’: the file is put away in the records and nothing further is done with it.

Original wording

“Op maandag 12 juni 2000 werd door u aangifte gedaan ter zake van smaad/laster. Naar aanleiding hiervan is er onderzoek verricht. Hiervan is proces-verbaal opgemaakt dat geregistreerd is onder nummer PL1523/1999/ 16079. Na bestudering van het proces-verbaal deel ik u mede dat het onderzoek geen resultaat heeft opgeleverd en derhalve zal worden “opgelegd” in de administratie.” Line breaks in the scan have been closed up. The quotation marks around ‘opgelegd’ are in the letter itself. In the running text the number of the official report has a space after the second slash; the letterhead writes the reference without one.

Why this document matters

With this letter of 19 September 2000, the criminal complaint of slander and libel that Giltay had filed on 12 June 2000 was closed. The letter is on the letterhead of the Haaglanden police and is signed, on behalf of the public prosecutor, by an assistant public prosecutor (hulpofficier van justitie), a police inspector. It states that the investigation has produced no result and is being ‘shelved’ in the records. Why it produced nothing, the letter does not say. Nor does it name the person against whom the complaint was directed; according to the complaint itself, that was Barbara Overduyn, a former employee of the Military Intelligence Service.

In July 1999 the police had at first refused to record the complaint, for which the district chief offered his apologies in March 2000. A little over three weeks after this letter, on 14 October 2000, Giltay took the matter to Queen Beatrix. The letter itself points out that a complaint may be lodged with the Court of Appeal under Article 12 of the Code of Criminal Procedure. Giltay took that route as well: on 7 March 2001 his complaint about the non-prosecution of Overduyn was received by the Court of Appeal of The Hague, which dismissed it on 19 December 2002.
