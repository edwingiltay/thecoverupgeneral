Min­is­try of De­fence to Giltay, 6 June 2017

The passage

“You have complained twice in the past about the conduct of the MIVD. The first complaint was declared unfounded after an investigation by the Review Committee on the Intelligence and Security Services. The National Ombudsman was likewise of the opinion that your complaint was unfounded. The second complaint was subsequently not taken up because of the lapse of time. For that same reason the National ombudsman saw no grounds either to take up your complaint. Your letter does not contain any new grounds or facts on the basis of which a correction should be made or apologies offered to you. I therefore consider your case closed.” Page 1 of the reply, page 2 of the PDF, the second, third and fourth paragraphs, run together here. Translated from the Dutch; the original wording is below.

Original wording

“U heeft zich in het verleden twee maal over het handelen van de MIVD beklaagd. De eerste klacht is na onderzoek door Commissie van Toezicht voor de Inlichtingen en Veiligheidsdiensten ongegrond verklaard. De Nationale Ombudsman was eveneens van mening dat uw klacht ongegrond was. De tweede klacht is vervolgens vanwege het tijdsverloop niet in behandeling genomen. Ook de Nationale ombudsman heeft om die reden geen aanleiding gezien uw klacht in behandeling te nemen. Uw brief bevat geen nieuwe aanknopingspunten of feiten op basis waarvan er rectificatie zou moeten plaatsvinden of aan u verontschuldigingen aan te bieden. Ik beschouw derhalve uw zaak als afgedaan.” The letter names the oversight body as ‘Commissie van Toezicht voor de Inlichtingen en Veiligheidsdiensten’, the body generally known by its abbreviation CTIVD, and does so without a preceding article. It also writes ‘Ombudsman’ with a capital in one sentence and, two sentences later, ‘ombudsman’ without one. Both are reproduced here as they stand.

Why this document matters

In this letter of 6 June 2017 the Ministry of Defence rejects Giltay’s request of 10 May 2017 to correct the 1999 ministerial declaration of insanity and to apologise. It cites a CTIVD investigation and the Ombudsman report of 1999, both said to have declared his complaint about the MIVD unfounded, states that his letter contains no new grounds or facts, and concludes: “I therefore consider your case closed.”

Both pillars had already been invalidated by then: the Ombudsman report by the Court of Appeal of The Hague in 2016 and by the Ombudsman’s own letter of 2000, the CTIVD investigation by the Committee itself, which had written as early as 2009 that it had not investigated his complaint about the MIVD. Moreover, more than a year earlier Giltay’s key accuser, Barbara Overduyn, had left unchallenged in court documents his refutation of her claim that he had been dismissed from Casema, where he had worked as a Randstad temp.
