Na­tion­al Om­buds­man, Woo decision on the file of report 1999/507, 7 Feb­ru­ary 2024

The passage

“I decide to provide 30 documents to you. The information relates to a report from 1999. In view of the age of the information, this information was searched for in the physical rather than the digital archive. In addition to the documents from your file that you already have, 30 other documents were found that are not yet in your possession.” Page 1. The first sentence is a subheading in the decision; the three sentences after it open the paragraph below. Translated from the Dutch; the original wording is below.

Original wording

“Ik besluit om 30 documenten aan u te verstrekken. De informatie ziet op een rapport uit 1999. Gelet op de ouderdom van de informatie is in het fysieke in plaats van het digitale archief gezocht naar deze informatie. Naast de documenten die u al heeft uit uw dossier, zijn nog 30 andere documenten aangetroffen die nog niet in uw bezit zijn.” The subheading has no full stop in the decision. Line breaks in the scan have been closed up.

Why this document matters

In this decision of 7 February 2024, the National Ombudsman provides Giltay with thirty documents about report 1999/507 that were not yet in his possession. They were found in the physical archive. Giltay had asked for them on 1 September 2023; on 27 December 2023 the District Court of The Hague ordered the Ombudsman to decide within two weeks, on pain of a penalty payment. The decision apologises for the delay and announces a separate notice about the penalty payment forfeited. Under Article 5.5 of the Open Government Act (Wet open overheid, Woo), the documents were provided to Giltay alone and were not made public. The essay names this decision as the moment when what lay in the safe came to light. One of the thirty documents is the telephone note of 7 October 1999 about the screening at Defence, number 11 on the inventory list.
