ICTY, testimony of Johannes Rutten in the Karadžić trial, 28 November 2011

The passage

“The Colonel took a photo of the bodies while another soldier collected the identification cards that were scattered on the ground nearby. The Colonel and the soldiers were forced to abandon the identification cards and flee the area after shots were fired in their direction. The Colonel reported what he had seen to his higher-ups. When the Colonel ultimately returned to the Netherlands, he turned the film roll over to the Dutch army intelligence branch for developing. He was later informed that an error had occurred during the development process and that the photos were mis-developed.” Pages 21985 and 21986, from the summary of the witness statement that the prosecution read into the record. The line numbers running down the margin of the transcript have been left out. On page 21988 the witness was asked whether the summary was accurate and answered: “Yes, it was an accurate summary.”

Why this document matters

In this transcript of the open session of 28 November 2011 at the International Criminal Tribunal for the former Yugoslavia, in the trial of the Bosnian Serb leader Radovan Karadžić, the prosecution read the summary of Rutten’s evidence into the record: on his return to the Netherlands he turned the film roll over to Dutch army intelligence for developing, and he was later informed an error had occurred and the photos were mis-developed. Rutten, who on 13 July 1995 had photographed the bodies of executed Bosniaks by a stream near Potočari, confirmed under oath two pages further on that the summary was accurate.
