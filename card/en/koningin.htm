Exhibit · Letter

Queen’s Office to Giltay, 31 October 2000

The passage

“At the request of Her Majesty the Queen, I inform you that the Queen has received your letter of 14 October and has placed it for consideration in the hands of the Minister of the Interior and Kingdom Relations.” Page 1, the body of the letter. Translated from the Dutch; the original wording is below.

Original wording

“Op verzoek van Hare Ma­jes­teit de Ko­nin­gin bericht ik U, dat de Ko­nin­gin Uw brief van 14 ok­to­ber jl. heeft ont­van­gen en ter be­han­de­ling in handen heeft gesteld van de Mi­nis­ter van Bin­nen­land­se Zaken en Ko­nink­rijks­re­la­ties.”

Why this document matters

By the autumn of 2000 Giltay’s criminal com­plaint of slander and libel, in which he had also put on record what an MID employee had told him about the Sre­bre­ni­ca pho­to­graphs, had stalled. He took the matter to the head of state. This letter is the reply: the Queen had re­ceived his letter of 14 October. The essay calls this an intervention: rather than merely taking note of the letter, she placed it for handling in the hands of the Min­is­ter of the In­te­ri­or and King­dom Re­la­tions, who was responsible for the police, where the complaint had stalled.

The reply is dated one day after the Min­is­ter of De­fence had told Giltay that no further re­sponse would be given to any letters from him. On 14 December 2000 came the letter in which The Hague’s mayor Wim Deetman, replying to Giltay’s objection of 22 July 2000, declared, as the authority responsible for the police force, that the objection was well founded as regards a promise the station chief had not kept. The letter from the Queen’s Office is signed ‘voor deze’, on behalf of the Director, by a member of staff. The quo­ta­tion in the Voices section therefore names no person but the Queen’s Office itself.
