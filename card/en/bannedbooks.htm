Video of the launch at the Banned Books Museum, Tallinn, 7 April 2024

The passage

“Well, we don’t usually collect books which are banned because of the courts, you know, because somebody sued somebody, defamation, these kinds of reasons. Usually we don’t collect them here in the museum. But Edwin’s story is a little bit different because almost never do these bans get overturned. Usually the author says, ‘Ah, my book is banned. Can’t do really anything about it.’ Very few people try to change it. Very few people try to contest it. But Edwin did. Which makes him kind of unique in this museum. In fact, it’s the only example we have of this. The only example of a person successfully suing back and winning.” From the recording, 0:51 to 1:34, spoken by the museum’s director, Joseph Dunnigan; transcribed from the video’s audio. The museum later toned down that last claim: on 16 April 2024 Dunnigan asked for ‘the sole book’ in the draft press release to be replaced by ‘a very rare example’, because the museum avoids specific claims about legal cases, and Giltay amended the text of his press release accordingly.

Why this document matters

In this recording of 7 April 2024 Joseph Dunnigan, director of the Banned Books Museum in Tallinn, calls the book the only example in the collection of an author who successfully challenged a ban. This website uses the wording he later approved, ‘a very rare example’. The essay calls the combination of a reversed total ban and the judicial rehabilitation of the author virtually unequalled. The edition presented in Tallinn is the free English translation, funded by the penalty payments the National Ombudsman made in February 2024. The museum had already added the Dutch edition to its collection in March 2021, according to a news item by Giltay in which Dunnigan says he is very happy to have the book on display. The press release on the launch, which Dunnigan read beforehand, states that he was presented with the first copy of the English edition in Tallinn.
