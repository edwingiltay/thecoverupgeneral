Min­is­try of De­fence to Giltay, 7 January 2008

The passage

“In your letter of 1 September 2007 you request the restoration of your good name. Your earlier requests for rehabilitation or rectification were answered by letters of 26 September 2000 and 30 October 2000. A copy of those letters is enclosed. Since you put forward no new facts or circumstances in your letter of 1 September 2007, I confine myself to a reference to the letters of 26 September and 30 October 2000. I have nothing to add to the contents of those letters.” Page 1, the body of the letter, which consists of this one paragraph. Translated from the Dutch; the original wording is below.

Original wording

“In uw brief van 1 september 2007 verzoekt u om eer­her­stel. Bij brieven van 26 september 2000 en 30 oktober 2000 is gereageerd op uw eerdere verzoeken om re­ha­bi­li­ta­tie danwel rec­ti­fi­ca­tie. Een af­schrift van deze brieven treft u hierbij aan. Nu u in uw brief van 1 september 2007 geen nieuwe feiten of om­stan­dig­he­den aanvoert, volsta ik met een ver­wij­zing naar de brieven van 26 september en 30 oktober 2000. Aan de inhoud van deze brieven heb ik niets toe te voegen.” The letter spells ‘danwel’ as one word, where standard Dutch has ‘dan wel’. The Dutch ‘eerherstel’ has no single English equivalent; it is rendered here as the restoration of one’s good name.

Why this document matters

In this letter of 7 January 2008 the Sec­re­tary-Gen­er­al of the Min­is­try of De­fence answers, on the Minister’s behalf, Giltay’s registered letter of 1 September 2007. It treats that letter only as a request for the res­to­ra­tion of his good name, states that Giltay puts forward no new facts or cir­cum­stan­ces, and points him back to two letters from 2000. The answer came four months later, after Giltay had twice asked the National Om­buds­man to in­ter­vene.

Yet the 2007 letter raised new matters. According to Giltay, the wife of Gen­er­al Ad van Baal, then Deputy Chief of the De­fence Staff, had been a temporary worker in the same Casema department as Overduyn and had written a report there on her colleague’s breaches of state secrecy. Giltay had turned to Queen Beatrix in October 2000 and had afterwards been found to be in the right in complaints procedures. And on 12 November 2004 the In­spec­tor-Gen­er­al of the Armed Forces, at the time Van Baal himself, had written to him that Overduyn made her remark entirely on her own account, and referred him to the Min­is­ter for the res­to­ra­tion of his good name. The answer takes up none of these points, nor the question whether the Minister had already seen the photo­graphs of the serious failure in Sre­bre­ni­ca.
