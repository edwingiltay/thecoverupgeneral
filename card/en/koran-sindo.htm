Koran SINDO on banned books, 15 Oc­to­ber 2018

The passage

“In 2015 a Dutch spy claimed that she had been misrepresented in this book. The book was later banned in the Netherlands by court order. Giltay denied having provided false information, and in 2016 the ban was overturned by The Hague Court of Appeal, one of the five courts of appeal in the Netherlands.” From the entry ‘The Cover-Up General (Edwin Giltay)’, second paragraph. Translated from the Indonesian; the original wording is below.

Original wording

“Pada 2015, seorang mata-mata Belanda mengklaim bahwa ia adalah salah diwakili dalam buku ini. Belakangan, buku itu dilarang di Belanda oleh perintah pengadilan. Giltay ditolak memberikan informasi palsu, dan pada 2016 larangan itu dicabut oleh Pengadilan Banding Den Haag, salah satu dari lima pengadilan banding di Belanda.” The article gives the title as ‘The Cover-Up General’. The Indonesian sentence ‘Giltay ditolak memberikan informasi palsu’ is a faulty rendering of ‘Giltay denied having provided false information’, and has been translated for sense. The Indonesian pronoun ‘ia’ has no gender; it is rendered as ‘she’ because the person meant is the former MID employee.

Why this document matters

Koran SINDO, an Indonesian daily, placed The Cover-up General on its list of books banned in the 21st century, alongside The Da Vinci Code, Persepolis and The King Never Smiles. The entry states that a Dutch spy (Barbara Overduyn, whom the article does not name) claimed in 2015 that she had been misrepresented in the book, that the book was then banned in the Netherlands by court order, and that Giltay denied having provided false information. It adds that the Court of Appeal of The Hague overturned the ban in 2016. The list shows that news of the Dutch ban also reached readers on the other side of the world.
