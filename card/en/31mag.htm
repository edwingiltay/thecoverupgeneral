31 Mag inter­views Giltay, 12 July 2016

The standfirst

“Cover-ups, censorship and the shadow of a genocide that might have been preventable: the author of De Doofpot Generaal tells 31mag.nl his story” Page 1, the standfirst below the headline of the article. The magazine writes the book title as ‘De Doofpot Generaal’. Translated from the Italian; the original wording is below. De doof­pot­ge­ne­raal is the book’s original Dutch title; the English edition is The Cover-up Gen­eral.

“Insabbiamenti, censure e l’ombra di un genocidio, forse, evitabile: l’autore di De Doofpot Generaal, racconta a 31mag.nl la sua storia” Original wording

The opening paragraph

“A roll of film with unpublished pictures taken during the Srebrenica massacre vanished into thin air. A senior figure in the Dutch army who wants to cover up inconvenient truths and two clumsy secret agents spying on each other; a complaint for calumny first upheld and then returned to sender by the Court of Appeal. The ingredients of a thriller are all there, except that the author, Edwin Giltay, didn’t have to make anything up.” Page 2, the first three sentences of the opening paragraph of the article, directly below the byline. The quotation on the website joins the standfirst to the third sentence. Translated from the Italian; the original wording is below.

“Un rullino fotografico con immagini inedite scattate durante il massacro di Srebrenica svanito nel nulla. Un papavero dell’esercito olandese che vuole insabbiare verità scomode e due maldestri 007 che si spiano a vicenda; una denuncia per calunnia prima accolta e poi respinta al mittente dalla corte d’appello. Gli ingredienti di un thriller ci sono tutti, se non fosse che l’autore, Edwin Giltay, non ha dovuto inventare niente.” Original wording

Why this document matters

On 12 July 2016 the Italian-language weblog 31 Mag explained the case to Italian-speaking readers: the Srebrenica film roll that vanished, the two intelligence officers watching each other, and a book that was withdrawn from circulation and then released again on appeal. This interview is one of more than four hundred publications about the book.
