Exhibit · Letter

Ministry of Defence to Giltay, 26 Sep­tem­ber 2000

The passage

“I have taken note of the fact that you have since filed a criminal complaint of slander and libel. Now that this matter has been placed in the hands of the judicial authorities, it is not possible for me to respond substantively, other than to refer to what was stated on this point during the proceedings before the National Ombudsman. In view of the above I see no reason to consider your request for rehabilitation or rectification.” Page 2, the two closing paragraphs of the letter. Translated from the Dutch; the original wording is below.

Original wording

“Van het feit dat u inmiddels aangifte heeft gedaan wegens smaad en laster heb ik kennis genomen. Het is mij niet mogelijk, nu deze zaak in handen is gesteld van Justitie, inhoudelijk te reageren, anders dan dat ik verwijs naar hetgeen hieromtrent tijdens de behandeling bij de Nationale ombudsman is gesteld. Gelet op het vorenstaande zie ik geen aanleiding uw verzoek om rehabilitatie c.q. rectificatie in overweging te nemen.” The two paragraphs follow one another directly and are run together here.

Why this document matters

In this letter of 26 September 2000 the Ministry of Defence declines to consider Giltay’s request of 21 August 2000 for rehabilitation or rectification. For most of his questions the Ministry refers him to the National Ombudsman’s report of 17 December 1999. It cannot respond substantively to the criminal complaint of slander and libel now that the matter is in the hands of the judicial authorities. The essay states that the ministries named in the book have never responded substantively to its revelations, and gives the letter of 30 October 2000 as the earliest instance. The October letter refers back to this one, saying the Ministry had nothing to add to it.
