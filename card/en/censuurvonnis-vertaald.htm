Exhibit · Judgment

District Court of The Hague, judgment of 14 December 2015, unofficial English translation

The passage

“orders the defendant to refrain from further distribution, publication and/or reprints of the book ‘De doofpotgeneraal’ and to remove the book from the internet and keep it off the internet, in so far as this lies within his power, and to refrain from further promotion of the book at lectures, book presentations and other public utterances, all on pain of forfeiture of a penalty payment of €1,000 for each day of non-compliance with this order, up to a maximum of €100,000;” Para. 5.1, the operative part of the judgment, running from page 7 on to page 8. It follows on from the line ‘The preliminary relief judge:’, which is why the sentence opens in lower case. Quoted here from the unofficial English translation; only the Dutch original is authoritative.

Why this document matters

This is the judgment that made The Cover-up General, as far as is known, the only Dutch book banned in its entirety and later released again by a court. On 14 December 2015 the preliminary relief judge of the District Court of The Hague prohibited not only distribution, publication and reprinting, but also any further promotion of the book at lectures, book presentations and other public utterances, on pain of a penalty payment of up to 100,000 euros. The ban thereby extended beyond the book itself and touched the author’s freedom to speak about his own life. Since the whole book was banned, this included the epilogue by former NOS editor-in-chief Hans Laroes as well. In April 2016 the Court of Appeal of The Hague overturned the ban.
